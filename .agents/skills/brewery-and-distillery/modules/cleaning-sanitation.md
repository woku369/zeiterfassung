# Cleaning and Sanitation

**Confidence**: High
**Last validated**: 2025-01-27
**Sources**: Five Star Chemicals (PBW, Star San product documentation), Craft Beer & Brewing, Brew Your Own, Northern Brewer, HomeBrewTalk, Adventures in Homebrewing, Hazy and Hoppy, Spike Brewing, Homebrewing DIY

## 1. The Cardinal Rule

**Cleaning and sanitizing are two separate, sequential steps.**

- **Cleaning** removes visible soils, oils, organic residue, and biofilms from equipment surfaces
- **Sanitizing** reduces bacteria, wild yeast, and other microorganisms to levels where brewing yeast can dominate fermentation

You cannot sanitize a dirty surface. Organic residue harbors microbes and shields them from chemical sanitizers. The sequence is always: clean first, sanitize second.

---

## 2. Hot Side vs. Cold Side

The boil is the dividing line between equipment that needs sanitizing and equipment that does not.

### Hot Side (Pre-Boil) — Clean Only

Everything that contacts wort before or during the boil does not require chemical sanitization because boiling temperatures (212°F / 100°C) kill microorganisms.

| Equipment | Sanitation Required? | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| Mash tun | No | Clean thoroughly after use to prevent mold growth |
| Brew kettle | No | The boil sanitizes; just keep it clean |
| Hot liquor tank | No | Handles only hot water |
| Sparge equipment | No | Water is near-boiling |
| Immersion wort chiller | No | Place in boiling wort 15 min before flameout |
| Mash paddle / spoon | No | Only contacts hot wort |

### Cold Side (Post-Boil) — Clean AND Sanitize

Once wort drops below ~160°F, it becomes susceptible to contamination. Everything that contacts cooled wort or finished beer must be sanitized.

| Equipment | Sanitation Required? | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| Fermenter | Yes | Hot soak with cleaner after every batch, then sanitize |
| Airlock and stopper | Yes | Soak in sanitizer solution |
| Siphon / racking cane | Yes | All tubing and canes |
| Hydrometer and test jar | Yes | Never return sampled wort to fermenter |
| Bottling bucket and wand | Yes | Including spigot |
| Bottles and caps | Yes | Soak in sanitizer |
| Kegs and fittings | Yes | Including posts, dip tubes, lids |
| Counterflow / plate chiller | Yes | First cold-side equipment; critical to sanitize |
| Yeast handling equipment | Yes | Flasks, stir plates, foil — maximum caution |

**Counterflow and plate chillers** deserve special attention. They are the first piece of cold-side equipment wort contacts, and their internal surfaces are difficult to inspect. Run hot wort through them for 5-10 minutes at end of boil, or recirculate hot PBW solution followed by sanitizer before use.

## 3. Cleaning Products

### PBW (Powdered Brewery Wash)

PBW is the industry-standard alkaline cleaner used by commercial and home breweries. It is a buffered alkaline detergent based on sodium percarbonate (similar to OxiClean) with added water softeners.

**Key specifications:**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| pH (1% solution) | 12 |
| Active ingredient | Sodium percarbonate + water softeners |
| Foam above 100°F | None (suitable for CIP) |
| Mixed solution life | 8-10 hours |
| Safety | Safe on skin, stainless steel, aluminum, plastics |
| Environmental | Biodegradable, septic-safe |

**Concentration and usage:**

| Application | Concentration | Temperature | Contact Time |
|-------------|--------------|-------------|--------------|
| Kettles, heavy soiling | 1-2 oz/gal | 140-160°F (60-71°C) | 30 min hot soak |
| Fermenters, kegs, cold-side | 3/4 oz/gal | 140-160°F (60-71°C) | 25-35 min hot soak |
| General homebrew | 2 oz / 5 gal | Any | Depends on temp |
| Overnight cold soak | 1-2 oz/gal | Room temp | Overnight (8-12h) |
| CIP circulation | 1-2 oz/gal | 130-180°F | 30+ min |

**Key points:**
- PBW is a cleaner only; it does NOT sanitize
- Hot water (140-160°F) activates the sodium percarbonate for maximum effectiveness
- Cold soaking overnight is effective but slower
- Eliminates scrubbing — soak and rinse
- Always rinse thoroughly after PBW before sanitizing
- Will not scratch or damage plastic fermenters (unlike abrasive scrubbing)

### OxiClean Free (Budget Alternative)

OxiClean Free (unscented, no dyes) is sodium percarbonate without PBW's water softeners. It works well as a cleaner at similar concentrations (1-2 oz/gal) but may leave mineral deposits in hard water areas. Use the **Free** version only — regular OxiClean contains fragrances that can taint beer.

### Bar Keepers Friend / Baking Soda

Useful for spot-cleaning stainless steel kettles and removing stubborn stains. Not a substitute for a full alkaline cleaning cycle.

### Unscented Dish Soap

A small amount of unscented dish soap works for light cleaning. Rinse extremely thoroughly — soap residue destroys head retention.

## 4. Sanitizing Products

### Star San

Star San is the most widely used no-rinse acid sanitizer in homebrewing. It combines two active ingredients — dodecylbenzenesulfonic acid and phosphoric acid — to create a low-pH environment lethal to microorganisms.

**Key specifications:**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Dilution | 1 oz per 5 gallons of water |
| Working pH | 2.5-3.5 (depending on water alkalinity) |
| Contact time | 30 seconds (per Five Star) to 5 minutes (EPA label) |
| Effective below pH | 3.5 (ceases to sanitize above this) |
| Rinse required | No — food safe at proper dilution |
| Concentrate shelf life | 1-2 years |

**Mixed solution shelf life:**

| Condition | Duration |
|-----------|----------|
| Official (Five Star) | Use immediately; max 1 hour |
| Sealed, distilled/RO water | Up to 3 months |
| Sealed, tap water | 3-4 weeks if pH stays below 3.5 |
| Open bucket, tap water | Use same day |

**Cloudiness:** Hard water causes phosphoric acid to react with calcium/magnesium, forming a visible cloud. As of Five Star's 2018 guidance, cloudiness alone does not indicate loss of effectiveness — pH is the definitive test. Use distilled or RO water to avoid cloudiness.

**Practical tips:**
- **Spray bottle method**: Fill a spray bottle with properly diluted Star San for quick sanitizing of surfaces, lids, spoons, scissors, and other small items
- **Never rinse** after Star San — it breaks down into yeast nutrients (phosphoric acid)
- Star San foam is not harmful and will not affect beer flavor
- Do not soak soft metals or plastics for extended periods (hours) — acid can cause damage over time. Stainless steel and glass tolerate prolonged contact
- Test pH with strips or meter when reusing solution — discard if above 3.5

### Iodophor

Iodine-based no-rinse sanitizer. Effective alternative to Star San.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Dilution | 12.5 ppm (typically 1/2 oz per 5 gallons) |
| Contact time | 2 minutes minimum |
| Rinse required | No at proper dilution |
| Color indication | Amber = active; clear = depleted |
| Staining risk | Can stain plastic at high concentrations |

### Heat Sanitization

| Method | Temperature | Time | Best For |
|--------|-------------|------|----------|
| Boiling water | 212°F (100°C) | 10 min | Metal equipment, glass |
| Oven (dry heat) | 350°F (177°C) | 60 min | Glass bottles |
| Dishwasher (heated dry) | 180°F+ | Full cycle | Bottles (no soap, heated dry cycle) |
| Steam | 212°F (100°C) | 15 min | Metal equipment |

**Caution:** Do not heat-sanitize plastic equipment. Heat can warp and degrade plastic fermenters.

## 5. The Two-Step Process

### Step 1: Clean

1. Disassemble equipment (remove keg posts, dip tubes, airlock components, spigots)
2. Prepare PBW solution at appropriate concentration in hot water
3. Soak equipment for 30 minutes (hot) or overnight (cold)
4. Scrub if necessary (use soft cloth or sponge — never abrasive pads on plastic)
5. Rinse thoroughly with clean water to remove all cleaner residue
6. Inspect visually — if residue remains, repeat

### Step 2: Sanitize

1. Prepare Star San solution (1 oz / 5 gal) or other sanitizer
2. Submerge or coat all surfaces that will contact cooled wort or beer
3. Allow minimum contact time (30 sec - 5 min for Star San)
4. **Do not rinse** — allow to drain and use immediately
5. Reassemble equipment just before use

### The Alkali-Acid Sequence

Following an alkaline cleaner (PBW, pH 12) with an acid sanitizer (Star San, pH 2.5-3.5) provides complementary action: alkaline cleaners dissolve organic soils while acid sanitizers remove mineral deposits and kill microbes. This one-two punch addresses both organic and inorganic contamination.

## 6. Equipment-Specific Procedures

### Fermenters (Plastic)

- Clean with PBW immediately after racking — don't let residue dry
- Never use abrasive scrubbers (scratches harbor bacteria in plastic)
- Replace plastic fermenters when scratched or stained
- Inspect for biofilm in scratches and seams

### Fermenters (Glass / Stainless)

- PBW soak dissolves krausen ring without scrubbing
- Glass carboys: use a carboy brush if needed
- Stainless conicals: CIP with hot PBW, drain, sanitize

### Kegs

1. Disassemble completely (posts, poppets, dip tube, O-rings, lid)
2. Soak small parts in PBW solution
3. Fill keg with PBW solution, let soak
4. Rinse all parts thoroughly
5. Reassemble with sanitized O-rings
6. Fill with Star San, pressurize with CO2, push Star San through liquid post to sanitize the entire liquid path
7. Push out Star San with CO2 — keg is now purged and sanitized

### Bottles

- Soak in PBW or OxiClean Free solution
- Use a bottle brush for stubborn residue
- Rinse thoroughly
- Sanitize with Star San on bottling day using a bottle tree and vinator (spray attachment)
- Or: oven sanitize at 350°F for 60 minutes (glass only)

### Draft Lines

- Flush with hot PBW solution every 2 weeks (minimum)
- Follow with water rinse, then Star San
- BLC (Beer Line Cleaner) is formulated specifically for draft lines and is more effective than PBW for beer stone and biofilm in lines
- Replace lines annually or when they develop persistent off-flavors

### Plate / Counterflow Chillers

- Backflush immediately after use with hot water
- Recirculate hot PBW solution (15-20 minutes)
- Rinse with clean water
- Before next use: recirculate Star San solution
- Disassemble and inspect periodically for blockages

## 7. Brew Day Sanitation Workflow

**Before brewing:**
1. All hot-side equipment should be clean from post-brew cleaning. Quick rinse is sufficient.
2. Prepare Star San solution in a bucket or spray bottle.
3. Sanitize all cold-side equipment: fermenter, lid, airlock, thermometer, siphon.

**Post-boil:**
1. Sanitize the outside of any hose connections to the chiller.
2. Sanitize fermenter if not already done.
3. Transfer cooled wort with minimal air exposure.
4. Sanitize yeast packet scissors, any additions.
5. Pitch yeast, seal fermenter.

**After brewing:**
1. Clean all hot-side equipment with PBW while still warm (easier cleaning).
2. Rinse and allow to dry completely before storage.
3. Store equipment clean and dry — mold grows on damp surfaces.

## 8. Troubleshooting Infections

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Prevention |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Pellicle (white film) on beer | Wild yeast or Lactobacillus | Better cold-side sanitation |
| Sour or vinegar taste | Acetobacter (needs oxygen) | Minimize headspace, check seals |
| Band-aid / medicinal | Chlorophenol (chlorine + wild yeast) | Remove chlorine from water (Campden tabs) |
| Ropy / viscous beer | Pediococcus | Replace scratched plastic, improve sanitation |
| Gushing bottles | Re-fermentation by wild yeast | Better bottle sanitation, proper priming |
| Mold on surface | Air exposure, incomplete seal | Check airlock, minimize headspace |

**If you get an infection:**
- Assess severity — mild sourness may be acceptable in some styles
- Glass and stainless equipment can be salvaged with aggressive cleaning and sanitization
- Plastic equipment that repeatedly produces infections should be replaced
- Tubing is inexpensive — replace it rather than risk re-infection

---

## 9. Quick Reference

### Do's
- Clean immediately after use (easier than cleaning dried residue)
- Sanitize everything that touches cooled wort or beer
- Use a spray bottle of Star San for quick sanitizing
- Test Star San pH when reusing old solution
- Replace tubing and gaskets regularly
- Store equipment clean and dry

### Don'ts
- Don't rinse after Star San (it's no-rinse for a reason)
- Don't use bleach (leaves residue, creates chlorophenols, corrodes metal)
- Don't use abrasive pads on plastic
- Don't skip cleaning and go straight to sanitizing
- Don't use scented soaps or cleaners
- Don't reuse Star San solution above pH 3.5
