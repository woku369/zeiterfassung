# Mead Science

*Synthesized from Ken Schramm's "The Compleat Meadmaker" (2003/2016)*

## Fermentation Fundamentals

### The Fermentation Equation

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
```

Yeast converts sugar (glucose) into ethanol and carbon dioxide. For this to occur optimally, yeast require:
- Sufficient water and sugar
- Adequate nutrients (nitrogen, vitamins, minerals)
- Appropriate temperature and pH
- Absence of oxygen (after initial aerobic phase)

### Four Phases of Fermentation

| Phase | Duration | What Happens |
|-------|----------|--------------|
| **Lag** | 12-24 hrs | Yeast acclimatizes, takes on nutrients, prepares for reproduction |
| **Respiratory (Aerobic)** | 1-3 days | Massive reproduction; yeast population multiplies 5x; oxygen is critical |
| **Fermentation (Anaerobic)** | 7-14 days | Sugar → alcohol + CO₂; population peaks at ~150 million cells/mL |
| **Flocculation** | Days-weeks | Yeast clump together and settle; fermentation slows |

## Nutrient Management

### The Critical Nitrogen Problem

Honey contains very little Free Amino Nitrogen (FAN)—far below the 130 mg/L minimum required for healthy fermentation.

**FAN Requirements:**
- Minimum: 130 mg/L
- Optimal: 300-500 mg/L
- Honey provides: Far below minimum (lighter honeys have least)

### Morse & Steinkraus Research (Cornell, 1966)

Groundbreaking study showing that proper nutrient supplementation enables mead fermentation to complete in **2 weeks** rather than months.

**Their findings:**
- Darker honeys ferment faster (higher natural nitrogen)
- Combined nitrogen + micronutrient formulas yield best results
- pH management is critical for fermentation speed

### Modern Nutrient Options

| Product | Contents | Dosage |
|---------|----------|--------|
| Fermaid K (Lalvin) | DAP + micronutrients | 1 g/gallon |
| Fermaid O | Organic nitrogen (YAN) | Per instructions |
| DAP (Diammonium Phosphate) | Nitrogen only | 1 tsp/gallon (~258 ppm FAN) |
| Yeast Energizer | Vitamins + micronutrients | Per package |
| Yeast Hulls | Cell wall material, lipids | 1 g/gallon |

**Best practice:** Use BOTH a nitrogen source (DAP or organic) AND a micronutrient source (energizer, yeast hulls).

### Staggered Nutrient Additions (TOSNA-style)

Rather than adding all nutrients upfront:
1. Add 1/3 at pitch
2. Add 1/3 at 24 hours
3. Add 1/3 at 48-72 hours (or when gravity drops ~30%)

This prevents nutrient toxicity and extends availability through fermentation.

## pH Management

### The pH Drift Problem

During the lag and aerobic phases, yeast absorb buffering compounds (minerals, amino acids) from the must. This causes pH to **drop**, sometimes to levels that inhibit fermentation.

**Critical thresholds:**
- Optimal for yeast: pH 3.7-4.6
- Below 3.0: Fermentation severely inhibited
- Some meads drop to pH 2.5 during fermentation

### pH Adjustment

**To raise pH (reduce acidity):**
- Add calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) ½ teaspoon at a time
- Stir, test, repeat until pH reaches ~3.8
- Avoid sodium bicarbonate (leaves salty taste)

**Important:** Do NOT add acid before fermentation completes. Acid additions for flavor balance should wait until after fermentation.

### Gluconolactone Effect

Honey contains gluconolactone, which converts to gluconic acid when pH is raised. This causes a "fading endpoint" in pH measurements—the pH will drift back down. Account for this in testing.

## Honey Science

### Honey Composition (USDA Technical Bulletin 1261)

| Component | Average % | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Water | 17.2% | Grade A/B must be ≤18.6% |
| Fructose | 38.2% | Primary sugar |
| Glucose | 31.3% | Secondary sugar |
| Sucrose | 1.3% | Broken down by invertase |
| Maltose | 7.3% | Complexity contributor |
| Higher sugars | 1.5% | Oligosaccharides |
| Acids | 0.57% | Primarily gluconic acid |
| Protein | 0.26% | Includes enzymes |
| Ash/minerals | 0.17% | Affects flavor, color |
| Nitrogen | 0.043% | Mostly bound (not FAN) |

### Honey Enzymes

1. **Invertase** — Converts sucrose → glucose + fructose
2. **Glucose oxidase** — Produces gluconic acid + hydrogen peroxide (antibacterial)
3. **Diastase** — Breaks down starches

All enzymes are **heat-sensitive** (destroyed above ~150°F/65°C).

### Color and Flavor Correlations

| Color | Mineral Content | Flavor | pH |
|-------|-----------------|--------|-----|
| Water white | Very low | Delicate, mild | Lower |
| Light amber | Low-moderate | Moderate character | Moderate |
| Dark amber | High | Strong, distinctive | Higher |

**Exceptions exist:** Basswood is very light but strongly flavored; tulip poplar is dark but mild.

### Honey Storage

| Temperature | Effect |
|-------------|--------|
| ≤0°F (-18°C) | Ideal—minimal degradation |
| 61-80°F | Short-term OK; no crystallization |
| 50-60°F | Crystallization zone—avoid |
| >80°F | Rapid deterioration of color, flavor, enzymes |

**Crystallization hazard:** Creates localized high-moisture areas where wild fermentation can occur → vinegar formation.

### Premium Mead Honeys

| Honey | Characteristics | Mead Notes |
|-------|-----------------|------------|
| **Orange blossom** | Light, very aromatic, citrus notes | Striking profile, delicious |
| **Tupelo** | High fructose, complex, low crystallization | Deep complexity, profound bouquet |
| **Raspberry** | High ash but exceptional aroma | Dynamite character, highly praised |
| **Sourwood** | High maltose, slightly high ash | Highly respected, complex |
| **Sage** | Light, delicate, inviting aroma | Clean, appealing |
| **Tulip poplar** | Dark but mild, high maltose | Distinctive, appealing |

### Honeys by Region

| Region | Notable Honeys |
|--------|----------------|
| Southeast US | Tupelo, sourwood, gallberry |
| Southwest US | Mesquite, sage |
| Northeast US | Raspberry, clover, wildflower |
| Pacific Northwest | Fireweed, blackberry, raspberry |
| Midwest | Clover, star thistle, tulip poplar |

### Honey Quality Indicators

**Positive signs:**
- Fresh extraction date
- Low moisture (<18%)
- Clear floral source documentation
- Pleasant aroma when tasted
- No off-flavors (smoke, fermentation, chemicals)

**Warning signs:**
- Crystallization with liquid separation
- Stale, vegetable-oil flavor (HMF degradation)
- Unknown age or origin
- Musty or barnyard aromas

## Yeast Selection

### Wine Yeasts for Mead

Wine yeasts are preferred over beer yeasts because:
- Mead sugars (glucose, fructose) match wine more than beer (maltose)
- Wine musts are similarly low in FAN
- Wine pH ranges (3.2-4.2) match mead
- Higher alcohol tolerance for stronger meads

### Recommended Strains

| Strain | Character | Alcohol | Best For |
|--------|-----------|---------|----------|
| **Lalvin 71B-1122** | Enhances fruit, softens acid, matures quickly | 14% | Melomels, fruit-forward meads |
| **Lalvin D-47** | Clean, takes longer to age | 12-14% | Traditional, medium-dry meads |
| **Lalvin EC-1118** | Very tolerant, strong fermenter, neutral | 18% | High-gravity, restarts stuck fermentations |
| **Lalvin K1V-1116** | Competitive, retains fruit character | 16-18% | Melomels, sparklers |
| **Red Star Côtes des Blancs** | Slow, accents fruit, residual sweetness | 12-15% | Sweet meads |
| **Red Star Pasteur Champagne** | Quick, high tolerance, very dry | 16%+ | Dry meads, sparkling |

### Rehydration Protocol

For dry yeasts (Lalvin, Red Star):
1. Use 2 oz water per 5g yeast at recommended temperature (95-109°F)
2. Add yeast to water (not reverse)
3. Do NOT stir initially
4. Wait 15 minutes
5. Stir well to suspend
6. Pitch when must is below 80°F

### Starter Protocol (Liquid Yeasts)

Target: ~6-7 oz yeast slurry for 5-gallon batch

1. Prepare starter must:
   - 6 cups water
   - ¼ tsp yeast energizer + nutrient
   - 1 tbsp dry malt extract
   - ½ cup honey
2. Boil 5 minutes, cool to room temperature
3. Transfer to sanitized 2L bottle
4. Add yeast, shake vigorously
5. Airlock and wait 12-24 hours
6. Optional: Decant and add fresh starter for larger slurry

### Gravity Targets by Style

| Style | OG | FG | Notes |
|-------|-----|-----|-------|

| Standard | 1.080-1.100 | 1.000-1.015 | Balanced |

| Sweetness | Final Gravity |
|-----------|--------------|
| Dry | 0.990-1.006 |
| Medium | 1.006-1.015 |
| Sweet | 1.012-1.020 |
| Dessert | 1.020+ |

### Honey-to-Gravity Rule

**1 pound honey per gallon raises gravity ~35 points (0.035)**

| Honey (5-gal batch) | Approx. OG |
|---------------------|------------|
| 10 lbs | 1.070 |
| 12 lbs | 1.084 |
| 15 lbs | 1.105 |
| 18 lbs | 1.126 |

**Honey weight:** ~11.75 lbs per gallon at 18% moisture

## Troubleshooting

### Fermentation Never Starts

| Cause | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Gravity too high (>1.140) | Dilute with water; repitch high-gravity-tolerant strain |
| Preservatives in juice | Cannot fix—start over without preservatives |
| Sanitizer residue | Cannot fix—ensure thorough rinsing |
| Dead yeast | Repitch fresh, properly rehydrated yeast |

### Prolonged Slow Fermentation

| Cause | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Insufficient nutrients | Add 1 g/gal nutrient + energizer |
| Insufficient oxygen | Stir vigorously or blend portion to aerate |
| Low temperature | Move to warmer location (65-75°F) |

### Stuck Fermentation

| Cause | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Low pH | Add CaCO₃ to raise pH above 3.5 |
| Yeast alcohol tolerance reached | Repitch EC-1118 or Champagne yeast |
| Stratification (layers formed) | Rack to remix; stir with racking cane |
| Temperature dropped | Warm to 68-72°F |

### Post-Fermentation Adjustments

**Too dry:** Add honey after stabilizing with potassium sorbate (½ tsp/gal)

**Too acidic:** This is rare; wait—mead often mellows with age

**Too flat/flabby:** Add acid blend ½ tsp at a time until balanced

## Mead Styles

### Traditional Mead (Show Mead)

Honey, water, and yeast only. Character comes entirely from honey variety.

### Melomel (Fruit Mead)

| Subtype | Fruit |
|---------|-------|
| Cyser | Apples/cider |
| Pyment | Grapes |
| Morat | Mulberries |
| Rubamel | Raspberries |
| Generic melomel | Any other fruit |

**Fruit addition:** 2-4 lbs/gallon in secondary fermentation

### Metheglin (Spiced Mead)

Herbs and/or spices added. Classic additions: cinnamon, clove, ginger, vanilla, nutmeg.

**Approach:** Add spices in secondary; taste weekly; remove when balanced.

### Braggot (Honey + Malt)

Hybrid of mead and beer. Must contain both honey and malted grain.

## Aging

### Why Mead Needs Time

Young mead often tastes:
- "Hot" (harsh alcohol)
- Angular or unbalanced
- Sharp or acidic
- Lacking complexity

With proper aging:
- Harsh edges smooth
- Flavors integrate and deepen
- Honey character develops
- Balance emerges

### Aging Timelines

| Style | Minimum | Optimal |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Hydromel | 3-6 months | 6-12 months |
| Standard | 6-12 months | 1-2 years |
| Sack/dessert | 1 year | 2-5+ years |
| High-alcohol (>14%) | 1-2 years | 3-10+ years |

### Storage Conditions

- Cool: 50-60°F ideal
- Dark: Light degrades quality
- Stable: Avoid temperature fluctuations
- Horizontal: Keep cork moist (if corked)
