## Fermentation Fundamentals

### What Is Fermentation?

Fermentation is the metabolic process where yeast converts sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide:

**C6H12O6 → 2 C2H5OH + 2 CO2**
(Glucose → Ethanol + Carbon Dioxide)

This anaerobic process produces:
- **Ethanol**: The alcohol we're after
- **CO2**: Provides natural carbonation
- **Heat**: Fermentation is exothermic (raises temperature)
- **Flavor compounds**: Esters, phenols, higher alcohols

### Yeast Biology Basics

**Saccharomyces cerevisiae** (ale yeast) and **Saccharomyces pastorianus** (lager yeast) are the primary brewing yeasts.

**Life cycle in fermentation:**
1. **Lag phase** (0-12h): Yeast acclimates, builds sterols/fatty acids for cell membranes
2. **Growth phase** (12-24h): Exponential reproduction, oxygen consumed, krausen forms
3. **Fermentation phase** (1-7 days): Sugar conversion to alcohol, CO2 production peaks
4. **Flocculation** (end): Yeast clumps and settles, fermentation slows
5. **Conditioning**: Yeast reabsorbs byproducts (diacetyl, acetaldehyde)

**Key yeast needs:**
- **Oxygen**: At pitch only (aerates wort for cell membrane synthesis)
- **Nutrients**: Nitrogen (FAN), minerals, vitamins (adequate in all-grain wort, may supplement for mead/wine)
- **Temperature**: Strain-specific optimal range
- **pH**: 4.0-5.5 optimal for most strains
- **Sugar**: Fermentable sugars (glucose, fructose, maltose, maltotriose)

### Temperature Control

Temperature is the single most controllable variable affecting fermentation quality.

**Effects of fermentation temperature:**

| Temperature | Effect |
|-------------|--------|
| Too cold (<50°F ales, <45°F lagers) | Yeast dormancy, stuck fermentation |
| Low optimal range | Cleaner profile, fewer esters, slower fermentation |
| High optimal range | More esters, faster fermentation, potential fusel alcohols |
| Too warm (>75°F most strains) | Fusel alcohols, stressed yeast, off-flavors |

**Temperature management strategies:**
- Swamp cooler (fermenter in water bath with frozen bottles)
- Fermentation chamber (chest freezer + temperature controller)
- Ambient basement/cellar (if naturally cool and stable)
- Insulation + heating pad (for maintaining warmth in cold climates)

### Pitch Rate and Yeast Health

**Pitch rate** = number of yeast cells per milliliter per degree Plato

**Standard rates:**
- Ales: 0.75 million cells/mL/°P
- Lagers: 1.5 million cells/mL/°P (double ales)
- High-gravity (>1.080): Increase rate by 25-50%

**Calculating pitch:**
```
Cells needed = (mL of wort) × (°Plato) × (pitch rate)

Example: 19 liters (19,000 mL) at 15°P for ale
= 19,000 × 15 × 0.75 = 213 billion cells
```

**Yeast viability:**
Fresh liquid yeast (Wyeast, White Labs): ~100 billion cells
Viability decreases ~20% per month from manufacture date
Always check date and calculate actual viable cells

**Making a yeast starter:**
1. Boil 100g dry malt extract (DME) per 1 liter water
2. Cool to ~70°F, sanitize and transfer to flask
3. Pitch yeast, cover with foil or airlock
4. Use stir plate if available (doubles growth)
5. Grow 24-48 hours before pitching
6. Decant liquid, pitch slurry for cleaner flavor

## Beer-Specific Guidance

### The Brewing Process

**Mashing**: Converting grain starches to fermentable sugars

1. **Strike water**: Heat water to hit target mash temperature
   - Strike temp = Target + (grain mass × 0.2) / water mass × 40
2. **Mash rest**: Hold at temperature for enzyme activity
   - 148-152°F: Beta-amylase (fermentable maltose)
   - 154-158°F: Alpha-amylase (less fermentable dextrins)
   - 152-154°F: Balance of body and fermentability
3. **Conversion time**: 60 minutes typical; iodine test confirms
4. **Mash-out** (optional): Raise to 168°F to stop enzymes, improve flow

**Lautering and sparging**: Separating and rinsing sugars from grain

- **Vorlauf**: Recirculate first runnings until clear
- **Fly sparge**: Continuous rinse, maintain 1-2" liquid above grain
- **Batch sparge**: Multiple water additions, drain completely between
- **Stop sparging** when gravity drops below 1.010 (tannin risk)

**Boiling**: Sterilization, hop isomerization, protein coagulation

- **60-90 minutes** typical (longer for pilsners to drive off DMS precursor)
- **Vigorous rolling boil** essential for DMS reduction
- **Hop additions**: Bittering (60 min), flavor (15-30 min), aroma (0-5 min)

**Cooling**: Rapidly reduce temperature to pitching range

- Target: <70°F within 20-30 minutes
- Methods: Immersion chiller, counterflow, plate chiller
- Rapid cooling reduces DMS, improves cold break, limits contamination window

### Hop Utilization

**IBU estimation (Tinseth formula):**
```
IBU = (oz hops × %AA × Utilization × 7489) / gallons

Utilization factors (60-min boil):
- 60 min: 0.25
- 30 min: 0.17
- 15 min: 0.10
- 5 min: 0.05
- 0 min (whirlpool): 0.00-0.05
```

**Dry hopping**: Adds aroma without bitterness
- Add to fermenter after active fermentation subsides
- 1-7 days contact time (longer = grassy/vegetal risk)
- 1-4 oz per 5 gallons typical for IPAs

### Water Chemistry for Beer

**Key ions:**
- **Calcium** (50-150 ppm): Yeast health, enzyme activity, clarity
- **Sulfate** (50-300 ppm): Accentuates hop bitterness, dry finish
- **Chloride** (50-150 ppm): Enhances malt sweetness, fullness
- **Sulfate:Chloride ratio**: Guides balance (2:1 for hoppy, 1:2 for malty)

**Mash pH target**: 5.2-5.6 (measured at room temperature)
- Too high: Harsh, astringent, poor conversion
- Too low: Thin body, sharp, mineral taste

## Wine-Specific Guidance

### From Grape to Glass

**Crushing and destemming**: Break grape skins, remove stems
- Red wine: Ferment on skins (color, tannin extraction)
- White wine: Press immediately, ferment juice only
- Rosé: Brief skin contact (hours), then press

**Must treatment:**
- **Sulfite** (Campden): 50 ppm SO2 kills wild yeast/bacteria, add 24h before pitching
- **Acid adjustment**: Target pH 3.2-3.6 (add tartaric if too high)
- **Sugar adjustment** (chaptalization): Add sugar if Brix too low for target ABV

**Primary fermentation (5-14 days):**
- Punch down cap twice daily (reds)
- Temperature: 60-75°F depending on style (cooler = more aromatic)
- Complete when specific gravity drops to ~1.000

**Pressing**: Separate wine from skins/pulp
- Free-run juice (highest quality, least tannin)
- Light press, then hard press (more tannin, can be harsh)

**Malolactic fermentation** (optional):
- Converts malic acid (sharp, apple-like) to lactic acid (soft, creamy)
- Reduces acidity, adds complexity
- Common in reds, full-bodied whites (Chardonnay)
- Inoculate with Oenococcus oeni or allow natural MLF

**Aging:**
- Bulk age in carboy/tank with minimal headspace
- Rack (transfer) every 2-3 months to remove sediment
- Oak influence: Barrels, chips, spirals, staves
- Age 6-24 months depending on style

**Bottling:**
- Sulfite addition (25-50 ppm SO2) for preservation
- Fine if needed (bentonite, isinglass, gelatin)
- Bottle with minimal oxygen exposure

## Mead-Specific Guidance

### Mead Fundamentals

**Honey varieties and character:**
- **Wildflower**: Complex, varies by region
- **Clover**: Mild, clean, neutral
- **Orange blossom**: Citrus notes, aromatic
- **Buckwheat**: Strong, malty, robust

**Calculating honey for target gravity:**
- ~1.035 gravity points per pound per gallon
- 12-15 lb honey for 5-gallon dry mead (~14% ABV)

**Mead styles:**
- **Traditional**: Honey, water, yeast only
- **Melomel**: With fruit
- **Metheglin**: With spices/herbs
- **Cyser**: With apple juice
- **Braggot**: With malt (mead-beer hybrid)

### Nutrient Management

Honey lacks nutrients yeast need. **Staggered nutrient addition** prevents stressed fermentation.

**TOSNA (Tailored Organic Staggered Nutrient Additions):**
- Calculate total nitrogen needed based on gravity
- Divide into 4 additions: At pitch, 24h, 48h, 1/3 sugar break
- Use Fermaid-O or similar organic nutrient
- Degas before each addition (prevents foam volcano)

**YAN (Yeast Assimilable Nitrogen) targets:**
- Low gravity (<1.090): 150 ppm YAN
- Medium (1.090-1.120): 200 ppm YAN
- High (>1.120): 250-300 ppm YAN

**Degassing**: Essential for mead
- CO2 buildup stresses yeast
- Stir/swirl vigorously twice daily during active fermentation
- Use wine whip or drill-mounted stirrer

### Mead Patience

Mead requires time. Young mead tastes "hot" (alcohol burn), harsh, or one-dimensional.

**Aging timeline:**
- 1-3 months: Drinkable but rough
- 6-12 months: Smoothing, flavors melding
- 1-2 years: Excellent for traditional meads
- Higher ABV = longer aging needed

## Distillation Guidance (Educational)

**Legal note**: Home distillation of alcohol is illegal in many jurisdictions without proper permits. This information is educational. Users must comply with local laws.

### Distillation Principles

**How distillation works:**
- Alcohol boils at 173°F, water at 212°F
- Heating fermented wash causes alcohol to vaporize first
- Vapor collected and condensed = distillate
- Multiple distillations and/or reflux increases purity

**Types of stills:**
- **Pot still**: Simple, batch process, retains more flavor/congeners (whiskey, brandy, rum)
- **Reflux still**: Column with packing, higher purity, cleaner (vodka, neutral spirit)
- **Hybrid**: Adjustable between pot and reflux characteristics

### Making Cuts

Not all distillate is equal. **Cuts** separate desirable from undesirable fractions.

**Fractions in order of collection:**
1. **Foreshots** (first 1-2%): Methanol, toxic; always discard
2. **Heads** (next 20-30%): Acetone, aldehydes; harsh, solvent-like; discard or feint
3. **Hearts** (middle 30-50%): Ethanol, desirable flavors; keep
4. **Tails** (final 20-30%): Fusel oils, heavy congeners; can add body but harsh; discard or feint

**Cutting by taste/smell:**
- **Heads end**: Solvent smell fades, sweetness emerges
- **Hearts end**: Flavor thins, wet cardboard or oily notes appear

**Feints**: Heads and tails can be added to next wash and redistilled

### Proofing and Dilution

**Proof** = 2 × ABV (100 proof = 50% ABV)

**Dilution formula:**
```
V2 = V1 × (ABV1 / ABV2)

Example: Dilute 1L at 70% to 40%
V2 = 1 × (70/40) = 1.75L final volume
Add 0.75L water to 1L spirit
```

**Use distilled or RO water** for dilution (minerals can cloud spirit)

## Universal Troubleshooting Framework

### Stuck Fermentation Diagnosis

**Step 1: Check temperature**
- Is it in optimal range for yeast strain?
- Has it dropped significantly (yeast dormancy)?
- Warm gradually (1-2°F per day) to rouse yeast

**Step 2: Check yeast health**
- Was pitch rate adequate?
- Was yeast fresh/viable?
- Were nutrients available (especially for mead)?
- Consider repitching with fresh, high-attenuating yeast

**Step 3: Check fermentability**
- What was mash temperature? (>156°F = more dextrins)
- Is honey fully dissolved?
- Use forced fermentation test to determine limit

**Step 4: Check for contamination**
- Unexpected sourness, funk, or pellicle formation?
- If contaminated, assess severity and decide to finish/dump

### Off-Flavor Identification

| Descriptor | Compound | Cause | Prevention/Fix |
|------------|----------|-------|----------------|
| Green apple | Acetaldehyde | Young, stressed yeast | Condition longer, warmer |
| Butter | Diacetyl | Incomplete conditioning | Diacetyl rest: warm 3-5 days |
| Cooked corn | DMS | Short boil, slow cooling | 60+ min boil, rapid chill |
| Solvent/hot | Fusel alcohols | Fermented too warm | Lower ferment temp |
| Band-aid | Chlorophenol | Chlorine + wild yeast | Campden tabs for chlorine |
| Sulfur/eggs | H2S | Stressed yeast (lagers) | Time, healthy yeast |
| Sour/vinegar | Acetic acid | Acetobacter (oxygen) | Better sanitation, minimize air |
| Cider-like | Acetaldehyde | Green beer | Age longer |
| Clove/spice | 4-VG | Belgian/wheat strains | Expected; control with temp |
| Banana | Isoamyl acetate | Warm fermentation | Expected in some styles |
