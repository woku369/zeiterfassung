# Brewery and Distillery - Advanced Methodology

## 1. Fermentation Science Deep Dive

### Yeast Metabolism Pathways

**Aerobic phase** (first 12-24 hours):
- Oxygen used for sterol and fatty acid synthesis
- Essential for healthy cell membranes
- Target: 8-12 ppm dissolved oxygen at pitch
- Methods: Shaking, aquarium pump, pure O2 with diffusion stone

**Anaerobic fermentation**:
- Glycolysis: Glucose → 2 Pyruvate + 2 ATP
- Pyruvate → Acetaldehyde → Ethanol (regenerates NAD+ for continued glycolysis)
- CO2 released as byproduct

**Flavor compound production**:
- **Esters** (fruity): Produced from alcohol + organic acid; increase with warmer temps, lower pitch rates
- **Fusel alcohols** (hot, solvent): Higher alcohols; increase with high temps, low nutrients, stressed yeast
- **Phenols** (clove, smoke, band-aid): POF+ strains; temperature dependent
- **Diacetyl** (butter): Fermentation intermediate; yeast reabsorbs during conditioning
- **Sulfur compounds** (eggs, vegetal): Common in lagers; resolved with time and healthy yeast

### Sugar Fermentability Hierarchy

Yeast consume sugars in order of preference:

1. **Glucose** (most preferred): Simple sugar, rapid uptake
2. **Fructose**: Simple sugar, slower than glucose
3. **Sucrose**: Split to glucose + fructose by invertase
4. **Maltose**: Primary beer sugar (50-60% of wort); requires maltase
5. **Maltotriose**: Last to ferment; some strains struggle

**Unfermentable sugars**:
- **Dextrins**: Complex starches from high mash temps; contribute body
- **Lactose**: Cannot be fermented by *Saccharomyces*; adds sweetness
- **Maltodextrin**: Similar to dextrins; used for body in low-ABV styles

### Attenuation Factors

**Yeast strain**: Each strain has characteristic attenuation range (typically 70-85%)

**Mash temperature** (beer):
- 145-150°F: High fermentability, dry finish
- 152-154°F: Balanced
- 156-160°F: Low fermentability, full body, higher FG

**Fermentation temperature**: Warmer = more complete attenuation (within reason)

**Pitch rate**: Adequate yeast = complete fermentation

**Nutrition**: Especially critical for mead (honey lacks nutrients)

## 2. Water Chemistry Mastery

### Ion Roles in Brewing

| Ion | Function | Flavor Impact | Typical Range |
|-----|----------|---------------|---------------|
| Calcium (Ca²⁺) | Enzyme cofactor, yeast health, clarity | Neutral | 50-150 ppm |
| Magnesium (Mg²⁺) | Enzyme cofactor, yeast nutrient | Mineral/sour at high levels | 0-40 ppm |
| Sulfate (SO₄²⁻) | Accentuates hop bitterness | Dry, crisp, hoppy | 50-300 ppm |
| Chloride (Cl⁻) | Enhances malt perception | Full, sweet, round | 50-150 ppm |
| Sodium (Na⁺) | Flavor enhancement at low levels | Salty, harsh at high | 0-100 ppm |
| Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) | Buffering capacity, raises pH | Harsh at high | 0-200 ppm |

### Style Water Profiles

| Style | Ca | Mg | SO₄ | Cl | Na | Ratio |
|-------|----|----|-----|----|----|-------|
| Pilsner | 10 | 3 | 10 | 10 | 2 | 1:1 |
| Munich Lager | 75 | 18 | 10 | 2 | 1 | 5:1 Cl |
| Vienna Lager | 75 | 15 | 60 | 60 | 10 | 1:1 |
| London Bitter | 70 | 10 | 50 | 70 | 25 | 1:1.4 Cl |
| Dublin Stout | 120 | 4 | 55 | 60 | 12 | 1:1 |
| Burton Pale Ale | 275 | 40 | 640 | 35 | 25 | 18:1 SO₄ |
| West Coast IPA | 100 | 20 | 200 | 75 | 25 | 2.7:1 SO₄ |
| NEIPA | 100 | 20 | 75 | 150 | 25 | 1:2 Cl |

### pH Management

**Mash pH target**: 5.2-5.6 (measured at room temperature)

**Factors affecting mash pH**:
- Base water alkalinity (HCO₃⁻ raises pH)
- Grain bill (darker malts are more acidic)
- Water-to-grain ratio

**pH adjustment options**:
- **Lactic acid** (88%): ~0.1 pH drop per 1 mL per 5 gal mash
- **Phosphoric acid** (10%): Similar effectiveness, cleaner flavor
- **Acidulated malt**: 1% of grist ≈ 0.1 pH drop
- **Gypsum/CaCl₂**: Calcium lowers pH slightly through reactions

**Measuring pH**:
- Cool sample to room temperature (pH meter reads high when hot)
- Calibrate meter with fresh buffers
- ATC (automatic temperature compensation) helps but cooling is best

## 3. Mashing Science

### Enzyme Activity Windows

| Enzyme | Optimal Temp | Function | Effect |
|--------|--------------|----------|--------|
| Phytase | 86-126°F | Releases phosphates | Lowers mash pH |
| Beta-glucanase | 95-113°F | Breaks down gums | Improves lautering |
| Peptidase | 113-131°F | Produces amino acids | Yeast nutrition |
| Protease | 131-137°F | Breaks down proteins | Head retention, clarity |
| Beta-amylase | 140-150°F | Creates maltose | Fermentability |
| Alpha-amylase | 154-162°F | Creates dextrins | Body, mouthfeel |
| Limit dextrinase | 140-149°F | Debranches starches | Minor fermentability boost |

### Mash Schedules

**Single infusion** (most common):
- Strike at 152-154°F, hold 60 min
- Best for well-modified malts (most modern malts)

**Step mash**:
- 122°F protein rest (10-15 min) for undermodified or high-adjunct
- 145-148°F beta-amylase rest (20 min) for dry beers
- 156-158°F alpha-amylase rest (20 min) for full body
- 168°F mash-out (10 min)

**Decoction mash** (traditional German/Czech):
- Remove portion of mash, boil it, return
- Creates melanoidins (rich, malty character)
- Time-intensive but authentic for certain lagers

### Sparge Optimization

**Fly sparge**:
- Continuous rinse at 168-170°F
- Maintain 1-2" liquid above grain bed
- Stop when runnings hit 1.008-1.010 (tannin risk below)
- Duration: 45-90 minutes

**Batch sparge**:
- Drain completely, add sparge water, stir, vorlauf, drain
- Faster than fly, slightly lower efficiency
- Multiple batches improve extraction

**No-sparge** (parti-gyle):
- Collect first runnings only
- Higher gravity, richer flavor
- Second runnings can make separate session beer

## 4. Hop Science and Utilization

### Alpha Acid Isomerization

**Bittering** requires boiling to isomerize alpha acids:
- Heat converts alpha acids to iso-alpha acids
- Iso-alpha acids are bitter; alpha acids are not
- Longer boil = more isomerization (up to ~40% max utilization)

**Utilization factors**:
- Boil time (longer = more)
- Wort gravity (higher gravity = less extraction)
- Hop form (pellets slightly higher than whole)
- Kettle geometry (surface area exposure)

### Hop Timing Effects

| Addition Time | Primary Contribution | Utilization |
|---------------|---------------------|-------------|
| 90-60 min | Bitterness | 20-30% |
| 30-15 min | Bitterness + Flavor | 10-20% |
| 10-5 min | Flavor + Aroma | 5-10% |
| Whirlpool (170-180°F) | Aroma + Flavor | 0-10% |
| Whirlpool (<170°F) | Aroma | Minimal |
| Dry hop | Aroma only | 0% |

### Biotransformation

Yeast can transform hop compounds during fermentation:
- Monoterpene alcohols converted to more aromatic forms
- Glycoside-bound aromatics released
- Best achieved with dry hopping during active fermentation
- Contributes to "juicy" character in NEIPAs

### Hop Products

| Product | Description | Use Case |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Whole hops | Dried cones | Traditional, filter bed, larger equipment |
| Pellets (T-90) | Concentrated, processed | Standard homebrew, most efficient |
| Lupulin powder (T-45) | Pure lupulin glands | High aroma, less vegetal |
| Cryo hops | Cryogenically separated | Double concentrated, smooth bitterness |
| Hop extract | Liquid alpha acids | Precise bittering, no vegetal matter |
| Hop oil | Essential oils only | Late aroma, research applications |

## 5. Yeast Management Deep Dive

### Propagation and Starters

**DME starter ratio**: 10:1 (100g DME per 1L water) = ~1.040 gravity

**Starter procedure**:
1. Boil DME + water 15 min, cool rapidly
2. Transfer to sanitized flask, cover with foil
3. Pitch yeast, swirl or place on stir plate
4. Room temp (68-72°F), 24-48 hours
5. Optional: Cold crash, decant liquid, pitch slurry only

**Growth expectations**:
| Starter Method | Growth Factor |
|----------------|---------------|
| Simple (no agitation) | 1.2-1.5x |
| Intermittent shaking | 1.5-2x |
| Stir plate | 2-3x |
| Multiple stir plate steps | 3-4x+ |

### Yeast Washing and Harvesting

**Harvesting from fermenter**:
1. After racking beer, swirl yeast cake
2. Pour into sanitized jar with sterile water
3. Let settle 20 min
4. Decant top layer (clean yeast)
5. Repeat if needed to remove trub
6. Store cold (34-38°F) for up to 2-3 months

**Acid washing** (cleaning yeast):
- Lower slurry pH to 2.0-2.5 with phosphoric acid
- Hold 2 hours at cold temp
- Kills bacteria, most wild yeast survives poorly at low pH
- Not foolproof; best used with good sanitation practices

### Yeast Strain Selection Guide

| Style Family | Characteristics to Seek | Example Strains |
|--------------|-------------------------|-----------------|
| American Ale | Clean, neutral, high attenuation | US-05, WLP001, Wyeast 1056 |
| English Ale | Fruity esters, some residual sweetness | S-04, WLP002, Wyeast 1968 |
| Belgian Ale | Esters + phenols, spicy, complex | T-58, WLP500, Wyeast 1214 |
| German Lager | Ultra-clean, crisp, malt-focused | W-34/70, WLP830, Wyeast 2124 |
| Czech Lager | Soft, malty, slightly estery | WLP800, Wyeast 2001 |
| Wheat Beer | Clove + banana (German), or clean (American) | WB-06, WLP300, Wyeast 3068 |
| Saison | Dry, spicy, phenolic | Belle Saison, WLP565, Wyeast 3724 |
| Kveik | Hot fermentation (80-100°F), fast, clean-tropical | Voss, Hornindal, Lutra |

## 6. Wild and Sour Fermentation

### Mixed Fermentation Microbiology

**Brettanomyces** (wild yeast):
- Produces earthy, funky, fruity flavors
- Can ferment sugars *Saccharomyces* cannot
- Works slowly; patience required (months)
- Strains vary from subtle to intense

**Lactobacillus** (bacteria):
- Produces lactic acid (soft, yogurt-like sourness)
- Fast acting at warm temps (90-115°F)
- Inhibited by hops (use under 5 IBU for quick sour)
- Common in kettle souring

**Pediococcus** (bacteria):
- Also produces lactic acid
- Works slower, at fermentation temps
- Produces diacetyl as intermediate (Brett cleans up)
- Partner to Brett in traditional lambic

**Acetobacter** (bacteria):
- Produces acetic acid (vinegar)
- Requires oxygen
- Undesirable except in intentional vinegar production

### Sour Beer Methods

**Kettle sour** (fast, controlled):
1. Mash and lauter normally (low IBU wort)
2. Pasteurize wort, cool to 100-115°F
3. Pitch *Lactobacillus* (starter, or grains with natural lacto)
4. Hold warm 24-72h until target pH (3.2-3.5)
5. Boil to kill lacto, add hops, ferment normally
6. Result: Sour but without funk (Berliner Weisse, Gose)

**Mixed fermentation** (long, complex):
1. Ferment normally with *Saccharomyces*
2. Add Brett + Lacto + Pedio to secondary
3. Age 6-18+ months
4. Sample periodically; package when ready
5. Result: Complex, funky, sour (Flanders red, oud bruin)

**Spontaneous fermentation** (traditional lambic):
1. Cool wort overnight in open coolship
2. Wild microbes from environment inoculate
3. Transfer to barrels, age 1-3 years
4. Blend different ages (gueuze)
5. Result: Most complex wild ales; unpredictable

### Managing Contamination

**Dedicated equipment**: Brett especially is persistent
- Separate fermenters, tubing, airlocks
- Label clearly: "SOURS ONLY"
- Some brewers use glass only (easier to sanitize)

**Oxygen management**:
- Acetobacter needs O2; minimize headspace
- Long aging: check airlocks regularly

**pH and gravity monitoring**:
- Brett continues slowly; gravity may drop further over time
- pH stabilizes; sourness develops

## 7. Conditioning and Clarification

### Maturation Chemistry

**Diacetyl rest** (lagers especially):
- Raise temp to 65-68°F at end of fermentation
- Yeast reabsorbs diacetyl precursor (acetolactate)
- 3-5 days typically sufficient
- Essential for clean lager character

**Lagering**:
- Cold conditioning at 32-38°F for 4-8 weeks
- Yeast settles, flavors meld, harsh edges soften
- Sulfur dissipates; malt smooths

**Mead/wine aging**:
- Bulk aging on lees (fine lees) adds complexity
- Racking every 2-3 months reduces sediment
- Time is the primary ingredient

### Fining Agents

| Agent | Type | Target | Usage |
|-------|------|--------|-------|
| Irish moss | Seaweed | Proteins | 1 tsp, last 15 min boil |
| Whirlfloc | Carrageenan tablet | Proteins | 1 tab, last 10 min boil |
| Gelatin | Protein | Yeast, polyphenols | 1 tsp/5 gal, cold crash |
| Isinglass | Fish collagen | Yeast | Cask conditioning |
| Bentonite | Clay | Proteins (wine) | Pre-fermentation |
| Sparkolloid | Alginate | Yeast, particles | Post-fermentation |
| PVPP | Polymer | Polyphenols (haze) | Chill haze prevention |

### Cold Crashing

- Drop temperature to 32-38°F for 24-72 hours
- Yeast and proteins precipitate
- Dramatically improves clarity
- Requires closed system to prevent suck-back

### Filtration

| Filter Type | Micron | Removes |
|-------------|--------|---------|
| Coarse | 10-20 | Large particles |
| Medium | 5 | Yeast cells |
| Fine | 1 | Most microbes |
| Sterile | 0.45-0.5 | Bacteria, remaining yeast |

**Trade-off**: Filtration removes body and foam-positive proteins; use judiciously

## 8. Carbonation and Packaging

### Carbonation Methods

**Bottle conditioning** (priming sugar):
- Calculate sugar needed for target CO2 volumes
- Corn sugar: ~4g/L for ~2.5 volumes CO2
- Allow 2-3 weeks at room temp for carbonation

**Force carbonation** (kegs):
- Set regulator to target PSI for temp/CO2 level
- Shake or wait (shake: hours; wait: 1-2 weeks)

**CO2 volumes by style**:
| Style | CO2 Volumes |
|-------|-------------|
| British ale | 1.5-2.0 |
| American ale | 2.2-2.7 |
| German lager | 2.4-2.8 |
| Belgian ale | 2.5-4.5 |
| Hefeweizen | 3.5-4.5 |
| Still wine | 0 |
| Sparkling wine | 4.5-6.0 |

### Packaging Best Practices

**Minimize oxygen pickup**:
- Purge vessels and bottles with CO2
- Closed transfers under pressure
- Low-oxygen caps or crown caps with O2 absorbers

**Bottle/keg preparation**:
- Clean, then sanitize
- Inspect for damage
- Bottles: Check for chips, clean residue

**Fill levels**:
- Bottles: ~1" headspace
- Kegs: Leave room for foaming during carbonation

## 9. Distillation Advanced Topics

**Legal disclaimer**: Educational information only. Distillation requires permits in most jurisdictions.

### Understanding Congeners

**Congeners** = flavor compounds beyond ethanol

| Compound | Boiling Point | Character |
|----------|---------------|-----------|
| Acetaldehyde | 69°F | Green apple, harsh |
| Methanol | 149°F | Toxic; discarded in foreshots |
| Ethyl acetate | 171°F | Solvent, nail polish |
| Ethanol | 173°F | Primary target |
| Propanol | 207°F | Sweet, slightly harsh |
| Water | 212°F | Dilutes alcohol |
| Isoamyl alcohol | 269°F | Fusel, banana |
| Acetic acid | 244°F | Vinegar |
| Furfural | 323°F | Burnt, papery |

### Making Good Cuts

**Sensory cutting** (most important skill):
- Collect small jars during run
- Smell each; taste diluted
- **Heads end**: Solvent smell dissipates, sweetness emerges
- **Hearts end**: Flavor thins, oily/cardboard notes appear

**Temperature is only a guide**; sensory evaluation is definitive

### Reflux Theory

**Reflux column** increases purity:
- Rising vapor meets descending liquid (reflux)
- Lower-boiling compounds rise higher
- Higher-boiling compounds return to pot
- More plates/height = more separation = higher ABV

**Reflux ratio** = reflux returned : product taken
- Higher ratio = purer output, slower production

### Barrel Aging Principles

**New oak**: Strong vanilla, coconut, wood tannins (bourbon)
**Used oak**: Softer, more complex, previous spirit character

**Variables**:
- Time: Longer = more extraction (diminishing returns)
- Surface area: Smaller barrels = faster aging
- Climate: Temperature swings drive spirit in/out of wood
- Char level: 1 (light) to 4 (alligator char); affects flavor

**Angel's share**: Evaporation loss (~2-4% annually)

## 10. Quality Control and Analysis

### Essential Measurements

| Measurement | Instrument | When |
|-------------|------------|------|
| Specific gravity | Hydrometer, refractometer | Pre/post fermentation |
| pH | pH meter, strips | Mash, fermentation |
| Temperature | Thermometer, probe | Throughout |
| ABV | Calculation, ebulliometer | Post fermentation |
| Dissolved O2 | DO meter | At pitch (if available) |
| Color (SRM) | Spectrophotometer, visual | Post-boil |

### Forced Fermentation Test

Determines maximum attenuation (fermentability):
1. Collect ~200mL wort before pitching
2. Overpitch with healthy yeast (or use excess starter)
3. Ferment warm (75-80°F) on stir plate
4. Measure gravity when stable (24-48h)
5. This is your expected terminal gravity

If main batch stalls higher, issue is with fermentation conditions, not fermentability.

### Sensory Evaluation

**BJCP scoring approach**:
- Aroma (12 points)
- Appearance (3 points)
- Flavor (20 points)
- Mouthfeel (5 points)
- Overall impression (10 points)

**Common descriptors**:
- Malt: Bready, biscuity, caramel, roasted, chocolate, coffee
- Hops: Citrus, tropical, floral, spicy, herbal, piney, resinous
- Yeast: Clean, fruity, estery, phenolic, spicy, earthy
- Faults: Acetaldehyde, diacetyl, DMS, oxidation, astringent, metallic

## 11. Recipe Formulation Strategies

### Grain Bill Construction

**Base malt** (80-100%):
- Pilsner: Pale, grainy, delicate
- Pale/2-row: Clean, neutral, flexible
- Maris Otter: Biscuity, slightly richer
- Munich: Malty, bready
- Vienna: Lightly toasty

**Character malts** (5-20%):
- Crystal/Caramel: Sweetness, color, body
- Munich/Vienna: Malt depth
- Biscuit/Victory: Toasty, bread crust

**Specialty malts** (1-10%):
- Chocolate: Color, coffee/chocolate notes
- Roasted barley: Dry roast, coffee
- Black malt: Color, minimal flavor (high Lovibond)
- Wheat: Head retention, haze, mouthfeel
- Oats: Silky mouthfeel, haze

### Hop Bill Construction

**Bittering layer**:
- High-alpha, neutral varieties (Magnum, Warrior)
- Or character hops for lingering flavor (Chinook, Columbus)

**Flavor layer**:
- Mid-boil additions (15-30 min)
- Hop varieties with desired character

**Aroma layer**:
- Late kettle, whirlpool, dry hop
- Most aromatic varieties shine here

**IBU:OG ratio guidelines**:
| Style | Ratio Range |
|-------|-------------|
| Light lager | 0.3-0.5 |
| Pale ale | 0.6-1.0 |
| IPA | 0.7-1.0 |
| Imperial IPA | 0.8-1.2+ |
| Stout | 0.4-0.8 |
| Belgian strong | 0.3-0.6 |

### Yeast Pairing

Match yeast character to style:
- Clean fermentation → American/German strains
- Fruity complexity → English/Belgian strains
- Spicy phenolics → Belgian/Weizen strains
- Temperature tolerance → Kveik for hot, lager for cold

Consider:
- Attenuation (dry vs. sweet finish)
- Flocculation (clear vs. hazy)
- Alcohol tolerance
- Nutrient needs

## Style-Specific Methodology Guides

### American IPA

**Key parameters**: OG 1.056-1.070, FG 1.008-1.014, IBU 40-70, SRM 6-14

**Grain bill**: 90-95% pale/2-row, 5-10% light crystal or no crystal (NEIPA)

**Hop approach**:
- 60 min for base bitterness
- Heavy whirlpool and dry hop (2-4 oz each)
- American C-hops (Cascade, Centennial, Citra, Mosaic, Simcoe)

**Water**: High sulfate (150-250 ppm), moderate chloride (50-100 ppm)

**Yeast**: Clean American ale (US-05/WLP001) or slightly fruity for NEIPA

### German Pilsner

**Key parameters**: OG 1.044-1.050, FG 1.008-1.013, IBU 25-45, SRM 2-5

**Grain bill**: 100% German Pilsner malt

**Hop approach**:
- Noble hops (Saaz, Hallertau, Tettnang, Spalt)
- Bittering at 60 min, flavor at 15-20, aroma at 0-5

**Water**: Very soft, minimal minerals

**Mash**: Decoction traditional, or single infusion 148-150°F

**Yeast**: German lager strain at 48-52°F, 4-6 week lager

### Belgian Tripel

**Key parameters**: OG 1.075-1.085, FG 1.008-1.014, IBU 20-40, SRM 4-7

**Grain bill**: 85-90% Pilsner, 10-15% simple sugar (candi sugar)

**Hop approach**: European noble, restrained; bitterness balanced not assertive

**Yeast**: Belgian abbey strain (WLP530, Wyeast 3787)
- Start cool (64-66°F), let rise to 72-78°F for complexity

**Fermentation**: 3-4 weeks primary, 4-8 weeks conditioning

### Oaked Red Wine

**Key parameters**: Brix 23-26, pH 3.3-3.6, TA 5-7 g/L

**Grape**: Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah, etc.

**Process**:
1. Crush, sulfite (50 ppm), pitch yeast
2. Ferment on skins 7-14 days, punch down 2x daily
3. Press, complete fermentation
4. MLF if desired (softer acidity)
5. Oak 6-18 months (barrel or alternatives)
6. Fine, sulfite (30 ppm), bottle

**Oak**: Medium toast French oak for elegance; American for more vanilla
