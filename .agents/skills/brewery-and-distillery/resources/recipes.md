### West Coast IPA: Rushmore IPA

*Source: [AHA - Rushmore IPA](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/rushmore-ipa-grain/) by John Palmer (Big Brew 2017)*

A classic West Coast IPA with just enough caramel and Munich malt to add complexity.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (18.93 L) |
| OG | 1.059 |
| FG | 1.015 |
| ABV | 6.0% |
| IBU | 70 |
| SRM | 6 |

**Grain Bill:**
- 11 lb (5 kg) Pale ale malt
- 1 lb (454 g) Munich malt
- 0.5 lb (227 g) Crystal 40°L

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Nugget | 1.0 oz | 60 min | 13% |
| Cascade | 0.5 oz | 15 min | 6% |
| Amarillo | 0.5 oz | 15 min | 10% |
| Centennial | 0.5 oz | 15 min | 10.5% |
| Cascade | 0.5 oz | 0 min (whirlpool) | - |
| Amarillo | 0.5 oz | 0 min (whirlpool) | - |
| Centennial | 0.5 oz | 0 min (whirlpool) | - |
| Cascade | 0.5 oz | Dry hop (10 days) | - |
| Amarillo | 0.5 oz | Dry hop (10 days) | - |
| Centennial | 0.5 oz | Dry hop (10 days) | - |

**Yeast:** American ale yeast (US-05, WLP001, or Wyeast 1056) with 250 billion cell starter

**Process:**
1. Mash at 153°F (67°C) for 60 minutes
2. Mash out at 168°F (76°C)
3. Collect 7-gallon pre-boil volume
4. 60-minute boil with hop additions per schedule
5. Chill to 65°F (18°C), pitch yeast
6. Ferment at 65°F for 7-9 days
7. Rack to secondary, dry hop for up to 10 days at 68°F
8. Package at 2.5 volumes CO₂ (or 4 oz corn sugar for bottles)

### American Pale Ale: Jeffrey's APA

*Source: [AHA - Jeffrey's American Pale Ale](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/jeffreys-american-pale-ale/) by Jeffrey Orr, Oregon Brew Crew (Vancouver, WA). 2024 NHC Gold Medal, Category 11: American Pale Ale (75 entries).*

A competition-winning modern American pale ale that showcases cutting-edge hop techniques — mash hopping, hopstand, and massive dry hopping with concentrated lupulin products. Heavily hopped by classic APA standards, but representative of where the style is heading. Phantasm Thiol Powder and pressure fermentation push tropical fruit aromatics to the forefront.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (18.9 L) |
| OG | 1.058 |
| FG | 1.015 |
| ABV | 5.6% |
| IBU | 44 |
| SRM | 6.3 |
| Efficiency | 65.6% |

**Grain Bill:**
- 7.7 lb (3.49 kg) Great Western Premium 2-Row
- 1.5 lb (680 g) Baird's Maris Otter
- 1.25 lb (567 g) Durst Malz Munich 13.1°L
- 13 oz (369 g) Durst Malz wheat malt
- 10 oz (283 g) Briess Caramel 10°L

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Saaz | 0.8 oz (23 g) | Mash hop | 2.4% |
| Cascade | 0.6 oz (17 g) | 60 min | 9.3% |
| Cascade | 0.4 oz (11 g) | 15 min | 9.3% |
| Cascade | 1.7 oz (48 g) | Hopstand 10 min @ 171°F | 9.3% |
| Chinook Lupomax | 1.7 oz (48 g) | Hopstand 10 min @ 171°F | 18% |
| Sabro Lupomax | 1.7 oz (48 g) | Hopstand 10 min @ 171°F | 19.5% |
| Citra hop hash | 1.7 oz (48 g) | Dry hop 3 days | 15% |
| Mosaic hop hash | 1.7 oz (48 g) | Dry hop 3 days | 20% |
| Azacca | 0.8 oz (23 g) | Dry hop 3 days | 13.8% |
| Sabro Lupomax | 0.8 oz (23 g) | Dry hop 3 days | 19.5% |

**Additional Ingredients:**
- 1.5 oz (42 g) Phantasm Thiol Powder @ 15 min
- 1 tablet Whirlfloc @ 15 min

**Yeast:** Omega OYL-406 Little DIPA ale yeast (1.5 L starter for 5 gal)

**Water Chemistry:**
- RO base water
- Target profile: Ca 110, Mg 18, Na 12, Cl 50, SO₄ 96, HCO₃ 216 ppm
- Salt additions (5 gal): 3.8 g calcium chloride, 5.4 g chalk, 5.7 g Epsom salt, 5.4 g gypsum, 2.5 mL phosphoric acid (75%)

**Process:**
1. Mash at 153°F (67°C) for 60 minutes with Saaz mash hops
2. 90-minute boil with hop additions per schedule
3. Cool to 171°F (77°C), add hopstand hops, steep 10 minutes
4. Chill to 68°F (20°C), pitch yeast
5. Ferment at 68°F (20°C) at 15 PSI for 7 days (pressure fermentation)
6. Add dry hops, hold 3 days
7. Rack and carbonate to 2.4 volumes CO₂

**Key tips:**
- Phantasm Thiol Powder (derived from Sauvignon Blanc grape skins) provides thiol precursors that yeast biotransforms into intense tropical fruit aromatics — a cutting-edge technique winning competitions.
- Pressure fermentation at 15 PSI suppresses ester formation for a clean, hop-focused profile while allowing warmer fermentation temperatures.
- Mash hops (Saaz) contribute polyphenols for mouthfeel and haze stability without significant bitterness.
- Lupomax pellets are concentrated hop products with higher alpha acid and more lupulin per gram. If unavailable, increase regular pellet amounts by 50%.
- "Hop hash" is a concentrated lupulin product. If unavailable, substitute with regular T-90 pellets at roughly double the amount.

### Irish Dry Stout: Bru'n Dry Stout

*Source: [AHA - Bru'n Dry Stout](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/brun-dry-stout/)*

A classic dry stout emphasizing proper water chemistry for the style's characteristic tart, acidic finish.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5.5 gallons (20.8 L) |
| OG | 1.042 |
| FG | 1.012 |
| ABV | 3.9% |
| IBU | 36 |
| SRM | 31 |
| Efficiency | 76% |

**Grain Bill:**
- 6.5 lb (2.95 kg) Two-row pale malt
- 1.3 lb (590 g) Flaked barley
- 14 oz (397 g) Roast barley

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| East Kent Goldings | 1.5 oz (42 g) | 60 min | 5% |
| East Kent Goldings | 0.5 oz (14 g) | 15 min | 5% |

**Yeast:** Irish ale yeast (White Labs WLP004 or Wyeast 1084)

**Water Chemistry:**
- Use RO or distilled water
- Add ½ tsp gypsum and ½ tsp calcium chloride per 8 gallons
- Acidify to achieve mash pH 5.3-5.5

**Process:**
1. Mash pale malt and flaked barley at 146-150°F for 60 minutes
2. Add roast barley at end of mash (reduces acrid harshness)
3. Optional mash-out to 168°F
4. Sparge with acidified water (pH 5.2-5.7)
5. 60-minute boil
6. Ferment at 64-68°F

**Key tip:** Low-alkalinity water allows the roast barley to provide characteristic tartness without harshness.

### German Hefeweizen: Holier than Meow

*Source: [AHA - Holier than Meow Hefeweizen](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/holier-meow-hefeweizen/)*

Classic Bavarian wheat beer with signature banana and clove character.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (18.9 L) |
| OG | 1.048 |
| FG | 1.010 |
| ABV | 4.9% |
| IBU | 18 |
| SRM | 3.5 |

**Grain Bill:**
- 4.5 lb (2 kg) Pilsner malt
- 4.5 lb (2 kg) Red wheat malt

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Hallertau | 1.2 oz (34 g) | 60 min | 4% |

**Yeast:** Wyeast 3068 Weihenstephan Weizen OR White Labs WLP300 Hefeweizen
- Make 1.3 qt (1.2 L) starter 2-3 days ahead

**Process:**
1. Strike water ~160°F to mash in at 149°F (65°C)
2. Hold mash 60-75 minutes
3. Mash out at 168°F (76°C)
4. Collect ~5.9 gallons wort
5. 60-75 minute boil
6. **Key fermentation technique:**
   - Chill to 54°F (12°C)
   - Pitch yeast and aerate
   - Allow temperature to rise to 64°F (18°C) during fermentation
   - Rest 3-7 days after fermentation ends
7. Package with high carbonation: 9.5 oz (270 g) corn sugar for 4 volumes CO₂
8. **Use heavy bottles** (Belgian-style) to handle high carbonation

**Flavor notes:** Starting cool and allowing temps to rise produces balanced banana/clove. Starting warm emphasizes banana; cooler temps emphasize clove.

### New Zealand / Italian Pilsner: NZ Pils

*Source: [AHA - NZ Pils](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/nz-pils/) — Zach Kosslow, Pittsburgh PA, Three Rivers Alliance of Serious Homebrewers (TRASH). 2024 NHC Gold Medal, Category 3: Pilsner (194 entries — the largest NHC category).*

"A snappy, NZ-forward Pilsner." This gold-medal recipe represents the hottest trend in lager brewing: dry-hopped pilsner with New World hops. Whether called Italian Pilsner (Birra Italiana) or NZ Pilsner, the concept is the same — a bone-dry, brilliantly hopped lager showcasing fruity/tropical hop character against a pristine Pilsner malt backdrop. This version uses Nelson Sauvin and Motueka for gooseberry, lime, and white wine character, with cutting-edge additions including Abstrax hop terpenes and ALDC enzyme.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5.75 gallons (21.8 L) |
| OG | 1.053 (13°P) |
| FG | 1.012 (3°P) |
| ABV | 5.4% |
| IBU | 35 |
| SRM | 3 |
| Boil Time | 60 min |
| Efficiency | 80% |

**Grain Bill:**
- 12.5 lb (5.67 kg) Weyermann Pilsner malt (100%)

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Amarillo | 1 oz (28 g) | Mash | 6.4% |
| Southern Cross | 1 oz (28 g) | 20 min | 13.4% |
| Motueka | 1 oz (28 g) | Whirlpool 20 min @ 175°F | 7.9% |
| Nelson Sauvin | 1 oz (28 g) | Whirlpool 20 min @ 175°F | 11.7% |
| Motueka | 1.5 oz (42 g) | Dry hop 3 days | 7.9% |
| Nelson Sauvin | 1.5 oz (42 g) | Dry hop 3 days | 11.7% |
| Motueka | 1.5 oz (42 g) | Keg hop | 7.9% |
| Nelson Sauvin | 1.5 oz (42 g) | Keg hop | 11.7% |

*Total hops: ~10.5 oz (298 g) — massively hopped for a pilsner*

**Yeast:** Cellar Science Berlin Lager (2 packs, 24 g total)

**Water:** RO base. Ca 53, Mg 0, Na 15, Cl 68, SO₄ 67, HCO₃ 0 ppm
- Balanced chloride-to-sulfate ratio supports both hop flavor and malt softness

**Additional Items:**
- 0.5 g Brewtan B in mash + 0.5 g in boil @ 16 min (antioxidant)
- 2 mL phosphoric acid in mash + 3 mL in boil @ 60 min (pH control)
- 3.2 g Wyeast yeast nutrient @ 15 min
- 1 tablet Whirlfloc @ 15 min
- 0.5 dropper ALDC at dry hop addition (prevents diacetyl from hop creep)
- 2.5 mL Abstrax Hop Terpenes, S. Hemisphere Tropical @ packaging

**Process:**
1. Mash at 148°F (64°C) for 90 minutes with mash hops (use any old hops for polyphenol extraction)
2. Ramp to 162°F (72°C) for 10 min, then mash out at 170°F (77°C) for 5 min
3. Target mash pH 5.4-5.5 with phosphoric acid
4. Add additional phosphoric acid to kettle for final pH of ~5.2
5. 60-minute boil with hop additions per schedule
6. Cool wort to 175°F (79°C), add whirlpool hops, hold 20 minutes
7. Chill to 52°F (11°C), pitch 2 packs Berlin lager yeast
8. Ferment at 52°F (11°C) until terminal gravity
9. Add dry hops and ALDC enzyme, allow free rise to 55°F (13°C) for 3 days
10. Package with sulfited gelatin solution and Abstrax hop terpenes

**Key tips:**
- **100% Pilsner malt** — the grain bill is deliberately simple to let the hops shine. This is the essence of Italian/NZ Pilsner: hop-forward with zero malt distraction
- **Mash hops** (Amarillo) contribute polyphenols for mouthfeel without significant bitterness — a technique borrowed from NEIPA brewing
- **ALDC enzyme** (alpha-acetolactate decarboxylase) prevents diacetyl formation from hop creep during dry hopping — critical for clean lager character with heavy dry hopping
- **Abstrax hop terpenes** are isolated aromatic compounds added at packaging for aroma without vegetal matter or haze — cutting-edge competition technique
- **Keg hopping** (loose hops in the keg) provides ongoing fresh hop aroma on every pour — use a fine mesh filter on the dip tube
- **Brewtan B** is a gallotannin antioxidant that prevents premature oxidation — particularly important for hop-forward lagers where stale hop character is immediately noticeable
- **Italian Pilsner** vs **NZ Pilsner**: Italian Pilsner traditionally uses Saphir or other German/NZ hops and was pioneered by Birrificio Italiano in the 1990s. This NZ version uses Nelson Sauvin and Motueka for more intense tropical/white wine character. Either approach is valid for the style

### White Cream Stout: The Great Pretender

*Sources: Brulosophy "Great Deceiver" Golden Stout, Brewer's Friend blonde stout recipes, Keg Outlet white stout, HomeBrewTalk forum threads*

A golden-colored cream stout that delivers coffee, chocolate, and vanilla character through post-fermentation additions rather than dark malts. Flaked oats, flaked barley, and lactose provide the creamy body; cacao nibs, coffee beans, and vanilla deliver the stout illusion. Expect puzzled reactions when tasters encounter dark stout flavors in a pale gold beer.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (19 L) |
| OG | 1.060 |
| FG | ~1.018 |
| ABV | ~5.5% |
| IBU | ~25 |
| SRM | 5–7 (gold) |

**Grain Bill:**
- 9.0 lb (4.1 kg) Maris Otter pale malt
- 1.5 lb (680 g) Flaked oats
- 1.0 lb (454 g) Flaked barley
- 0.75 lb (340 g) **Lactose** (add to boil at 15 min)
- 0.5 lb (227 g) Victory malt (toasty, biscuit notes)
- 0.5 lb (227 g) Crystal 20°L
- 0.25 lb (113 g) Carapils (Dextrine malt)

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Fuggle | 1.25 oz (35 g) | 60 min | ~4.5% |
| East Kent Goldings | 0.5 oz (14 g) | 5 min | ~5.0% |

**Yeast:** English ale yeast (WLP002 English Ale, Wyeast 1968 London ESB, or Lallemand London ESB). English strains leave residual sweetness and body that complement the cream stout character. US-05/WLP001 works for a cleaner version.

**Post-Fermentation Additions** (add after primary fermentation is complete, ~5–7 days):
- 4 oz (113 g) **cacao nibs** — toast at 350°F (177°C) for 10 minutes, cool, then soak in 2–3 oz vodka for 24 hours. Add nibs and vodka to fermenter in a sanitized mesh bag
- 4 oz (113 g) **whole coffee beans** (medium roast) — add whole, unground, in a sanitized mesh bag. Do not crush; whole beans extract flavor without excessive bitterness or astringency
- 1 **vanilla bean** — split lengthwise, scrape seeds, soak bean and seeds in 1 oz bourbon or vodka for 1–2 weeks. Add tincture at packaging

**Contact Times:**
- Cacao nibs: 5–7 days (taste daily after day 3; beyond 7 days can turn bitter)
- Coffee beans: 24–48 hours only (coffee extracts quickly and turns acrid if left too long)
- Vanilla tincture: add at packaging for clean vanilla note

**Process:**
1. Mash at 154°F (68°C) for 60 minutes — higher mash temp for fuller body
2. Add rice hulls (0.5 lb) if concerned about a stuck sparge from the flaked grains
3. 60-minute boil; add lactose at 15 minutes remaining
4. Chill to 66°F (19°C), pitch yeast
5. Ferment at 66–68°F (19–20°C) for 7 days
6. Add cacao nibs in mesh bag; begin tasting at day 3
7. Add coffee beans in separate mesh bag 24–48 hours before packaging
8. Add vanilla tincture at packaging
9. Carbonate to 2.2–2.4 volumes CO₂. Serve on **nitro** if possible — the cascading pour and creamy head amplify the stout illusion

**Key tips:**
- **Toast the oats**: For extra depth, spread flaked oats on a baking sheet and toast at 350°F (177°C) for 25–30 minutes before brew day. This adds a subtle roast/biscuit note without color
- **Coffee bean selection**: A medium roast with chocolate or nutty tasting notes works best. Avoid dark/French roast, which can be harsh and ashy
- **Cacao nibs**: Buy raw, unsweetened nibs. The vodka soak sanitizes and extracts fat (which can harm head retention). Discard the vodka soak liquid or add it — brewer's choice
- **Water**: Target a chloride-forward profile (Cl:SO₄ ratio ~1.5:1) for malt sweetness emphasis. Add 4 g calcium chloride and 2 g gypsum per 5 gallons of total water
- **Avoid peat or smoked malt**: Any dark specialty grain defeats the purpose of this style

### Milkshake Stout: Chocolate Vanilla Milkshake Stout

*Source: Adapted from [Brewer's Friend — Milkshake Stout](https://www.brewersfriend.com/homebrew/recipe/view/1498511/milkshake-stout) by Buenos Amigos Beer, scaled to 5 gallons*

A decadent dessert stout applying the milkshake concept — lactose, vanilla, and chocolate — to a rich stout base. Silky, sweet, and deeply chocolatey with vanilla cream character. The flaked oats and lactose create a thick, creamy mouthfeel while chocolate malt, cacao nibs, and vanilla beans deliver layers of dessert complexity. The "milkshake" in a milkshake stout means lactose sweetness + vanilla + thick body, producing a beer that drinks like a chocolate milkshake with a roasty edge.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (19 L) |
| OG | 1.063 |
| FG | 1.023 |
| ABV | 5.2% |
| IBU | 36 |
| SRM | 69 |
| Boil Time | 60 min |

**Grain Bill** (scaled from 50 L original):
- 9.2 lb (4.17 kg) Pale ale malt (77%)
- 8.7 oz (246 g) CaraCrystal wheat malt (4.5%)
- 8 oz (227 g) Chocolate malt — late mash addition (4.2%)
- 4 oz (114 g) Blackprinz malt — late mash addition (2.1%)
- 8 oz (227 g) Flaked oats (4.2%)
- 3.6 oz (102 g) Roasted barley — late boil addition (1.9%)

**Lactose:**
- 12 oz (340 g) Lactose (milk sugar) — add at 15 minutes remaining in boil (6.1%)

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| East Kent Goldings | 1.3 oz (37 g) | 90 min | 6.2% |
| Cascade | 0.15 oz (4 g) | First wort | 5.8% |

**Yeast:** Fermentis Safale US-05 (or any clean American ale yeast). Ferment at 65°F (18.5°C).

**Vanilla & Chocolate Additions** (add to secondary or primary after fermentation):
- 2 vanilla beans — split, scraped, soaked in 2 oz vodka for 48 hours to make tincture
- 3 oz (85 g) cacao nibs — lightly toasted at 300°F for 10 minutes, then soaked in 2 oz bourbon or vodka for 48 hours
- Add both tinctures to beer 2 days before packaging

**Process:**
1. Mash at 154°F (68°C) for 60 minutes — higher temp preserves dextrins for thick body
2. Add chocolate malt and Blackprinz as late mash additions (last 15 minutes) to reduce astringency
3. Fly sparge at 168°F (76°C)
4. 60-minute boil; add roasted barley at 15 minutes remaining (reduces harsh roast)
5. Add lactose at 15 minutes remaining in boil
6. Chill to 65°F (18°C), pitch yeast
7. Ferment at 65°F for 10-14 days
8. Add vanilla tincture and cacao nib tincture, hold 2 days
9. Cold crash, package at 2.0-2.2 volumes CO₂

**Key tips:**
- **Late additions of dark malts** (chocolate, Blackprinz in the last 15 min of mash, roasted barley in the last 15 min of boil) extract color and flavor while minimizing harsh, acrid bitterness — a technique from award-winning stout brewers
- **Lactose is unfermentable** by Saccharomyces — it provides permanent sweetness and body that persists through fermentation
- **Vanilla tincture** gives more control than adding beans directly — you can dose to taste in a measured sample before adding to the full batch
- **Toast cacao nibs** before soaking to develop deeper chocolate flavor and reduce vegetal/tannic character
- **Bourbon-soaked nibs** add an extra dimension — the alcohol extracts chocolate oils while contributing subtle oak/vanilla/caramel notes
- **Mash at 154°F or higher** — a milkshake stout should be thick and full. Low mash temperatures will produce a thin, watery beer that contradicts the style's purpose
- **FG of 1.023** is intentional — between the lactose and the high mash temp, this beer should finish sweet and viscous

### Midwest Fruit Tart (Sherbet-Style Sour Template)

*Source: [AHA - Homebrewing a Midwest Fruit Tart Beer](https://homebrewersassociation.org/how-to-brew/homebrewing-a-midwest-fruit-tart-beer/)*

A template for intensely fruity, sherbet-like sour beers with tropical character.

| Parameter | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons |
| OG | 1.075-1.080 |
| FG | 1.012-1.015 |
| ABV | 7.5-9.0% |
| IBU | 15-25 (bittering only) |
| Fruit | 2.5-3 lb per gallon |
| Final pH | 3.2-3.4 |

**Base Grain Bill Options:**

*Option 1 (The Gadget):*
- 80% Two-row/Pale malt
- 20% Vienna malt

*Option 2 (Fire Iron):*
- 100% Two-row/Pale malt

**Fruit Combinations (examples for 5 gallons):**

*Berry Blend:*
- 7 lb Seedless raspberry puree
- 7 lb Seedless blackberry puree
- 1.5 lb Sugar

*Tropical Blend:*
- 8.75 lb Pink guava puree
- 2.5 lb Passionfruit puree
- 3 lb Banana puree
- 1 lb Sugar

**Kettle Sour Process:**
1. Mash at 148°F (maximize fermentability)
2. Collect only 4 gallons of wort (concentrated)
3. Heat to 180°F to pasteurize
4. Cool to 85°F (29°C)
5. Pitch pure Lactobacillus culture (NOT grain, yogurt, or GoodBelly for this style)
6. Seal vessel, ferment 48-72 hours until pH reaches ~3.4
7. Boil, add bittering hops (no aroma additions)
8. Chill, pitch Kveik yeast

**Fruit Addition Process:**
1. Ferment base beer 2-3 days at 86°F (30°C) with Kveik
2. At high kräusen, add fruit puree, sugar, and yeast nutrients
3. Continue fermentation 7+ days at up to 96°F (35°C)
4. Allow 2 weeks for fruit solids to settle
5. Package at 2.8-3.0 volumes CO₂

**Key tips:**
- Use only 100% fruit purees — no concentrates or flavorings
- Kveik yeast handles high temps and ferments fast
- Optional: 0.1g vanilla per gallon adds depth
- Pink guava and passionfruit are "loud" fruits; use less for subtlety
- The high fruit rate (2.5-3 lb/gal) creates the sherbet-like intensity

9. Clarify and adjust sweetness if desired
10. Bottle when clear

**Notes:** High-gravity fermentation requires healthy yeast and careful temperature management. Expect extended aging (12+ months) for best results.

### Modern Braggot: Brown Ale Base

*Source: [AHA - Braggot: The Best of Mead and Beer](https://homebrewersassociation.org/how-to-brew/braggot-the-best-of-mead-and-beer/) and [Home Brew Answers](https://homebrewanswers.com/making-a-braggot-recipe/)*

A modern approach to braggot—honey added to a proven beer recipe for a balanced hybrid.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (18.9 L) |
| OG | 1.075-1.085 |
| FG | 1.012-1.018 |
| ABV | 8-9% |
| IBU | 20-25 |

**Grain Bill:**
- 8 lb (3.6 kg) Maris Otter pale malt
- 1 lb (454 g) Crystal 60L
- 8 oz (227 g) Chocolate malt
- 4 oz (113 g) Victory/Biscuit malt

**Honey (add post-boil):**
- 2 lb (907 g) Varietal honey (wildflower, orange blossom, or clover)

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Fuggles | 1 oz | 60 min | 4.5% |

### Gruit: Benedictus Gruit Ale

*Source: [AHA - Benedictus Gruit Ale](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/benedictus-gruit-ale/) — Travis Rupp and Avery Brewing Co. (Zymurgy Nov/Dec 2018)*

A historically-informed gruit from Avery Brewing's Ales of Antiquity Series. Uses floor-malted grains, smoked malt, and a precise six-herb gruit blend. The Trappist yeast adds fruity complexity, and wheat straw filtering is an authentic touch. Significantly more sophisticated than a generic gruit.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (18.9 L) |
| OG | 1.065 (16°P) |
| FG | 1.016 (4°P) |
| ABV | 6.5% |
| IBU | 0 (no hops) |
| Style | Historical Beer / Gruit |

**Grain Bill:**
- 5.4 lb (2.45 kg) German floor-malted Bohemian wheat malt (45%)
- 3.6 lb (1.63 kg) German floor-malted Bohemian Pilsner malt (30%)
- 1.2 lb (544 g) German floor-malted Bohemian dark malt (10%)
- 1.2 lb (544 g) German smoked malt (10%)
- 9.6 oz (272 g) Acidulated malt (5%)

- 0.25 oz (7 g) Juniper
- 0.22 oz (6.25 g) Lavender
- 0.11 oz (3.25 g) Marjoram
- 0.10 oz (3 g) Wormwood
- 0.05 oz (1.5 g) Bay leaf
- 0.05 oz (1.5 g) Rosemary

### Ethiopian T'alla (Traditional Beer)

*Source: [Ethiopian Food Blog](https://ethiopianfood.wordpress.com/2011/02/18/talla-tell-al/)*

The traditional Ethiopian beer that forms the base for T'ej—made from barley, wheat, and gesho.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 1 gallon (3.8 L) |
| ABV | 2-4% |
| Style | Farmhouse grain beer |

**Ingredients:**
- 1 lb (454 g) Barley flour
- 8 oz (227 g) Whole wheat berries
- 1 oz (28 g) Gesho leaves or powder
- 1 gallon (3.8 L) Water

**Process (Traditional):**

*Day 1-3: Prepare gesho water*
1. Soak gesho leaves in 2 cups water for 3 days

*Day 1-4: Sprout wheat*
1. Soak wheat berries in water for 24 hours
2. Drain and spread on damp cloth
3. Keep moist, allow to sprout 2-3 days

*Day 4: Prepare wheat*
1. Toast sprouted wheat in dry pan until slightly crispy
2. Add to gesho water

*Day 4-5: Prepare barley*
1. Mix barley flour with water to form thick batter
2. Spread on baking sheet
3. Bake at 350°F until starting to dry (not fully baked)
4. Crumble into pieces

*Day 5+: Ferment*
1. Combine all components in fermentation vessel
2. Add remaining water
3. Ferment 3-5 days at room temperature
4. Strain through cloth when ready

**T'alla to T'ej conversion:**
- T'alla becomes T'ej when honey is added and fermented further
- Add 1-2 lb honey per gallon of T'alla
- Continue fermenting 1-2 weeks

**Key tips:**
- Complex multi-day process—plan ahead
- Gesho provides both fermentation culture and bitter balance
- Result is thick, slightly sour, with grain sediment

### Medieval Mead Recreation (14th Century English)

*Source: [AHA - Boiling & Seething: Recreating Medieval Mead Recipes](https://homebrewersassociation.org/beyond-beer/boiling-seething-recreating-two-medieval-mead-recipes/) from Curye on Inglysch*

Two 14th-century mead recipes with spiced variations.

**Recipe 9: Basic Medieval Mead**

Traditional honey-water fermentation using gentle heating.

**Ingredients:**
- Honeycomb pressings ("pomys")
- "Life honey" (raw, unpressed honey)
- Clean water

**Process:**
1. Press honeycomb to extract "life honey"
2. Simmer comb in water below hand temperature
3. Add life honey (approximately 1 quart per 5 gallons)
4. Let settle in sealed vessel for several days
5. Fermentation begins from ambient yeasts

**Key technique:** "Boyling" and "seething" in medieval texts meant heating below wax's melting point (154°F / 68°C), not actual boiling. This preserved wild yeasts while dissolving honey.

---

**Recipe 10: Fine and Piquant Mead (Spiced)**

A stronger, spiced mead using wort and spices.

**Ingredients (scaled to 5 gallons):**
- 4 gallons pressed honeycomb mead (from Recipe 9)
- 0.6 gallons fine wort (light malt tea)
- 0.2 gallons life honey
- Pepper (to taste)
- Cloves (to taste)

**Process:**
1. Heat all ingredients together until clear
2. Add back to the first mead's vessel for secondary flavoring
3. Age until clear and mellow

**Historical note:** These recipes appear in the earliest known English culinary manuscripts. Previous translations misidentified "pomys" as apples — it actually meant honeycomb pressings.

### Rauchbier: Smoked Heller Bock

*Source: [AHA - Smoked Heller Bock](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/smoked-heller-bock/) — Christopher Owen, Louisville KY, Louisville Area Grain and Extract Research Society (LAGERS). 2024 NHC Gold Medal, Category 30: Smoke-Flavored Beer (76 entries).*

Bold, uncompromising beechwood smoke "reminiscent of barbeque and charcuterie" with noble hop spiciness. This gold-medal recipe uses traditional German techniques: Kesselmaischen (kettle mashing), Hochkurz (two-rest saccharification), and Sauergut for Reinheitsgebot-compliant pH adjustment. The grain bill is 91% Rauchmalt — this is Bamberg in a glass.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (18.9 L) |
| OG | 1.067 (16.4°P) |
| FG | 1.012 (3°P) |
| ABV | 7.4% |
| IBU | 25 |
| SRM | 5 |
| Boil Time | 60 min |
| Efficiency | 81% |

**Grain Bill** (scaled from 13.21 gal original):
- 10 lb (4.54 kg) Bestmalz Rauchmalt (beechwood-smoked malt)
- 1 lb (454 g) Bestmalz Pilsner malt

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Saaz T45 | 1 oz (28 g) | 60 min | 8.4% |
| Saaz T45 | 0.1 oz (3 g) | 10 min | 8.4% |

*Note: Saaz T45 is a concentrated hop pellet. If using standard Saaz pellets, double the amounts.*

**Yeast:** Fermentis Saflager 34/70 (2 packs for 5 gal)

**Water:** Ca 45, Mg 0, Na 0, Cl 78, SO₄ 0, HCO₃ 0 ppm. Use CaCl₂ to reduce mash pH.

**Additional:** ~380 mL Sauergut at end of boil (scaled from 1 L for 13.21 gal)

**Process:**
1. **Kesselmaischen** (kettle mashing): Mash 80% of the grain bill (8 lb Rauchmalt + all Pilsner) in the kettle
2. Perform a decoction, then add remaining 20% of grain (2 lb Rauchmalt) after the decoction
3. **Hochkurz mash**: Rest at 150°F (66°C) for beta-amylase activity, then raise to 160°F (71°C) for alpha-amylase
4. Mash out at 170°F (77°C)
5. Boil 60 minutes with hop additions per schedule
6. Add Sauergut at end of boil to lower knockout pH
7. Chill and pitch Saflager 34/70
8. Classic cold fermentation at 50-54°F (10-12°C)
9. Cold secondary fermentation (lagering) at 33-35°F (1-2°C) for 4-6 weeks
10. **Spund** (cap fermenter) to naturally carbonate to 2.5 volumes CO₂

**Key tips:**
- **Bestmalz Rauchmalt** is beechwood-smoked — the traditional Bamberg smoke character. Weyermann Rauchmalz is an acceptable substitute
- **Kesselmaischen** (kettle mashing) heats the mash directly in the kettle rather than using infusion — gives more precise control over decoction temperature steps
- **Hochkurz** (short-high) skips the protein rest in favor of two saccharification rests: 150°F converts fermentable sugars, 160°F preserves dextrins for body
- **Sauergut** is biologically acidified wort (Lactobacillus culture) — the traditional German method for pH adjustment compliant with Reinheitsgebot. If unavailable, substitute 2-3 mL food-grade lactic acid
- The grain bill is 91% Rauchmalt — for a subtler smoke character, reduce to 50-70% Rauchmalt and increase Pilsner malt proportionally
- **Spunding** (capping the fermenter during active fermentation to naturally carbonate) produces the finest, most traditional carbonation
- Serve at 45-50°F (7-10°C) — pairs brilliantly with smoked meats, aged cheese, and grilled sausage

### Mexican Lager: The Most Interesting Beer in the World

*Source: Zymurgy Magazine — Recipe by Amahl Turczyn*

A crisp, clean, supremely drinkable light lager in the Mexican tradition. The Vienna malt nods to the style's 19th-century Austrian roots — when Habsburg-era brewers emigrated to Mexico and brought Vienna lager techniques that evolved into the Mexican lager we know today. Flaked maize provides the characteristic dry, corn-sweet lightness. Think Negra Modelo's lighter cousin.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5.5 gallons (20.8 L) |
| OG | 1.048 (11.9°P) |
| FG | 1.009 (2.3°P) |
| ABV | 5.1% |
| IBU | 20 |
| SRM | 3.2 |
| Boil Time | 90 min |

**Grain Bill:**
- 6 lb (2.72 kg) Pilsner malt
- 2 lb (0.9 kg) flaked maize (corn)
- 1.5 lb (0.68 kg) Vienna malt

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% | IBU |
|------|--------|------|-----|-----|
| Tettnang | 1 oz (28 g) | 60 min | 4.5% | 15 |
| Saaz | 1 oz (28 g) | 10 min | 4% | 5 |

**Yeast:** Mexican lager yeast (2 L starter) — White Labs WLP940 Mexican Lager or equivalent

**Water:** RO or distilled base. Add 1 g/gal (0.3 g/L) calcium chloride — intentionally minimal mineral profile for clean, soft character.

**Process:**
1. Mash at 148°F (65°C) for 60 minutes
2. Apply heat or boiling water to raise to 168°F (76°C) over 20 minutes
3. Hold at 168°F (76°C) for 10 minutes to mash out
4. Sparge at 168°F (76°C)
5. Boil 90 minutes with hop additions per schedule
6. Chill to 50°F (10°C) and oxygenate
7. Pitch a strong starter of yeast
8. Ferment at 50-55°F (10-13°C) until terminal gravity is reached
9. Raise to 60°F (16°C) and hold 2 days for diacetyl rest
10. Crash to 35°F (2°C) and lager at 35-40°F (2-4°C) for at least one month

**Extract version:** Substitute 6 lb (2.72 kg) Pilsner malt extract syrup for Pilsner and Vienna malts, and 1 lb (0.45 kg) dextrose for flaked maize. Omit calcium chloride. Dissolve extract and sugar completely in RO water, top off to desired boil volume, and proceed as above.

**Key tips:**
- **WLP940 Mexican Lager** is the classic choice — descended from the original yeast strains brought to Mexico by Austrian/German brewers. Fermentis W-34/70 is a workable substitute
- **Flaked maize** (not cornmeal or grits) provides the clean corn-sweet character and lightens body without a cereal mash — it's pre-gelatinized and can go directly into the mash
- **Vienna malt at 16%** of the grain bill connects this beer to its historical Vienna lager roots — it provides the subtle biscuit/toast complexity that distinguishes Mexican lager from generic American light lager
- **90-minute boil** drives off DMS precursors from the Pilsner malt — essential for a clean lager
- **Minimal water chemistry** (just calcium chloride on RO) produces the soft, clean character — avoid sulfate, which would sharpen the bitterness inappropriately for this style
- **Lager for at least one month** — patience is the hardest ingredient. Two months is even better. Serve ice cold (35-40°F) with lime

- **Three-yeast blend**: S-04 does the heavy lifting; tiny amounts of WB-06 and T-58 add complexity and fruity esters without farmhouse character. Exceeding the specified amounts of WB-06 and T-58 will add too much phenolic/farmhouse flavor

- **Minimize oxygen** at every step after fermentation — NEIPAs are extremely sensitive to oxidation (brown color, cardboard off-flavor)

### Brut IPA: Brutal Fall Off a Bike

13. After 6 days, drop to **38°F (3°C)**

15. Add **12 ml Biofine Clear**, agitate with CO₂
16. After 48 more hours, **closed transfer** to CO₂-purged keg
17. Add **0.6 ml Strata terpenes** dissolved in ~2 ml Everclear to keg
18. Carbonate to **3.5 volumes CO₂**

- **pH management is critical** — the enormous dry hop charge raises pH significantly; starting low at each stage ensures the finished beer doesn't taste harsh or astringent

- **Mash hops at 148°F** extract hop polyphenols that drive biotransformation during fermentation, without contributing significant IBUs
- **Brewtan B** at mash and boil prevents premature oxidation — important for preserving the pale straw color and clean hop aromatics
- **Strata terpenes at kegging** add aroma intensity without introducing vegetal matter or risking oxidation from additional dry hopping
- **Bone-dry finish (FG 1.007)** is characteristic of Brut IPA — the ALDC and simple grain bill (all Pilsner malt + dextrose) ensure full attenuation

*Total hops: ~13.93 oz (395 g) including pellets, Lupomax, Cryo, and Incognito — Citra-forward with Simcoe and Mosaic supporting*

**Yeast:**
- Imperial Flagship ale yeast (2.5 L starter)

**Water:**
- RO base water
- Target: Ca 79 ppm, Mg 11 ppm, Na 0 ppm, Cl 70 ppm, SO₄ 139 ppm
- Salts in mash: 6.5 g gypsum (CaSO₄), 5.5 g calcium chloride (CaCl₂), 4.2 g Epsom salt (MgSO₄), 2 mL lactic acid

- 1 tablet Whirlfloc at 10 min
- Gelatin finings at kegging

2. Mash at **152°F (67°C)**, target pH **5.2**
3. Fly sparge **2.64 gal (10 L)** RO water for **40 minutes**
4. Preboil volume: **7.66 gal (29 L)**
5. Boil **120 minutes** to final volume ~6.87 gal (26 L)
6. At flameout, whirlpool hop additions at **180°F (82°C)** for **5 minutes**
7. **"North Park Style" Citra Incognito:** pump 180°F wort into two sanitized heat-safe pitchers, squeeze Citra Incognito into both pitchers
8. Chill remaining kettle wort, pump cold into fermenter
9. Pour hot pitchers with Incognito directly into fermenter
10. Oxygenate wort, pitch yeast at **60°F (16°C)**
11. Slowly warm to **64°F (18°C)** over 2 days, hold for 4–5 more days
12. Near terminal gravity, ramp **1°F every 12 hours** to **70°F (21°C)**
13. Hold until fermentation is complete and diacetyl is clear
14. Soft crash to **57°F (14°C)**, hold 1 day
15. Add all dry hops, hold **2 more days** (leave airlock intact)
16. Cold crash to **33°F (1°C)** for 1 day
17. Add gelatin to well-purged keg, rack beer
18. Carbonate to **2.4 volumes CO₂**

**Key tips:**
- **"North Park Style"** refers to a San Diego technique for using concentrated liquid hop products (Incognito) — dissolving them in hot wort in a separate vessel ensures even distribution without clumping
- **Citra Incognito** is a flowable (liquid) concentrated hop extract — if unavailable, substitute with 2 oz additional Citra pellets in the whirlpool
- **Lupomax and Cryo hops** are concentrated products with more lupulin per gram — if using standard pellets, increase amounts by 50%
- **The 120-minute boil** is essential for concentrating wort and developing melanoidins from the Pilsner malt base
- **Sulfate-forward water** (139 SO₄ : 70 Cl ratio) accentuates hop bitterness and crispness
- **Gradual temperature ramp** from 60°F to 70°F ensures complete attenuation (FG 1.008) without harsh fusel alcohols
- The brewer's advice: "Always enjoy a homebrew while brewing, for good luck"

### Cold IPA: Wayfinder Chronokinetic Clone

*Source: [Brew Your Own — "The Cool New Style: Cold IPA"](https://byo.com/article/the-cool-new-style-cold-ipa/) — Clone of Wayfinder Brewing Co.'s Chronokinetic Cold IPA (Portland, OR). Wayfinder's Kevin Davison is credited with inventing the Cold IPA style.*

The definitive Cold IPA from the brewery that invented the style. "Cold IPA hits with a strong punch of aromatic hop intensity and bitterness but finishes crisp and clean, leaving the drinker craving another sip." The key: lager yeast fermented warm (~65°F) on an adjunct-heavy grain bill with aggressive American hopping, yielding a crystal-clear, bone-dry, hop-forward beer that bridges the gap between IPA and lager.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (19 L) |
| OG | 1.065 |
| FG | 1.009-1.010 |
| ABV | 7.5% |
| IBU | 55 |
| SRM | 3 |

**Grain Bill:**
- 9.8 lb (4.45 kg) North American Pilsner malt
- 3.2 lb (1.45 kg) flaked corn grits
- 3 oz (85 g) acidulated malt

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Magnum | 0.2 oz (6 g) | 90 min | 14.5% |
| Hopshot Columbus extract | 3 mL | 90 min | — |
| Cashmere | 0.6 oz (17 g) | 10 min | — |
| Centennial | 0.6 oz (17 g) | 10 min | — |
| Cashmere | 0.6 oz (17 g) | 0 min (whirlpool) | — |
| Centennial | 0.6 oz (17 g) | 0 min (whirlpool) | — |
| Strata | 2.3 oz (65 g) | Dry hop | — |
| Centennial | 1.2 oz (34 g) | Dry hop | — |
| Motueka | 1.2 oz (34 g) | Dry hop | — |

**Yeast:** Fermentis SafLager W-34/70 (or Wyeast 2124 Bohemian Lager, LalBrew Diamond)

**Water:** Soft water (Pilsen profile target). 50 ppm calcium from CaSO₄ (~5 g gypsum per batch).

**Process:**
1. **Step mash** (critical for flaked corn conversion):
   - 144°F (62°C) for 45 minutes (beta-amylase — maximum fermentability)
   - 154°F (68°C) for 30 minutes (alpha-amylase)
   - 162°F (72°C) for 15 minutes (iodine test to confirm conversion)
   - 172°F (78°C) mash out
2. 90-minute boil with hop additions per schedule
3. Chill to 55°F (13°C), pitch lager yeast
4. Ferment at 65°F (18°C) — yes, this is warm for lager yeast; the style deliberately uses lager yeast's clean profile at ale temperatures
5. Day 7: Transfer to pressure vessel onto dry hops at 15 PSI
6. Continue 4 days at 65°F (18°C)
7. Cool to 32°F (0°C), add fining agents (gelatin or Biofine)
8. 5-7 days cold conditioning for clarity and carbonation
9. Package crystal clear — Cold IPA should be brilliantly clear, not hazy

**Key tips:**
- **Cold IPA ≠ India Pale Lager (IPL)**: Cold IPA uses adjuncts (corn/rice), ferments warm with lager yeast, and avoids Munich/Vienna malt. IPL is a traditional lager grain bill with dry hops
- **Flaked corn** (or rice) at 25% of the grain bill lightens body, increases fermentability, and creates the bone-dry finish that defines the style — do not substitute with specialty malts
- **Lager yeast at 65°F** sounds wrong but is the point — it produces a cleaner profile than ale yeast at the same temperature, with none of the fruity esters
- **Step mash** is recommended because the low-temperature beta-amylase rest maximizes fermentability for that critical dry finish (target FG below 1.010)
- **Hopshot Columbus** is a concentrated liquid hop extract for clean bittering — if unavailable, substitute 0.5 oz Columbus pellets at 90 min
- **Clarity is essential** — this is the anti-NEIPA. Use fining agents, cold crash, and if possible, filter. The beer should be straw-pale and crystal clear
- **Pressure fermentation** during dry hopping (15 PSI) suppresses additional ester formation and naturally carbonates

## Cider Recipes

### Quick & Easy Malted Cider

*Source: [AHA - Quick & Easy Malted Cider](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/quick-easy-malted-cider/)*

Malted wort provides yeast nutrients that apple juice lacks, creating a cider with pleasing body and complexity.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5.25 gallons (20 L) |
| OG | 1.050 (12°P) |
| FG | 1.004-1.006 (before backsweetening) |
| ABV | ~6% |

**Ingredients:**
- 4 gallons (15.1 L) Apple juice (1.050 gravity)
- 1 gallon (3.8 L) Malted barley wort (1.040-1.050 gravity)
- 2 tsp cream of tartar
- 2 twelve-oz cans frozen apple juice concentrate (backsweetening)
- English ale yeast

**Process:**
1. Boil 1 gallon wort tailings for at least 15 minutes
2. Pour apple juice into sanitized 6.5-gallon fermenter with splashing (aeration)
3. Rack wort onto juice
4. Equalize temp to ~68°F, pitch yeast
5. Ferment completely until clear
6. Rack to keg, backsweeten with concentrate to taste
7. Force carbonate, chill to 40°F before serving

**Note:** Not recommended for bottle conditioning with backsweetening (risk of overcarbonation).

## Additional Mead Styles

### Cyser: Take Me Back to Tupelo

*Source: [AHA - Take Me Back to Tupelo Cyser](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/take-me-back-to-tupelo-cyser/)*

A cyser (apple mead) using premium Tupelo honey—sweet, spicy, and worth the investment.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons |
| Style | Cyser (Apple Melomel) |

**Ingredients:**
- 3.5 gallons (13.2 L) Fresh sweet apple juice (chilled)
- 0.5 gallon (1.9 L) Apple juice (for heating)
- 12 lb (5.4 kg) Tupelo honey
- 1 tbsp yeast nutrient
- 2 packets Côte des Blancs yeast

**Process:**
1. Heat ½ gallon juice to 140°F (do NOT boil)
2. Dissolve honey into warm juice
3. Pour chilled juice into sanitized fermenter
4. Add honey mixture and yeast nutrient
5. Pitch yeast, seal fermenter
6. Ferment in dark, cool location (~60°F) for 2-4 weeks
7. Rack when fermentation completes and sediment settles
8. For carbonation: Prime with corn sugar (½ cup in ½ cup boiled water)
9. Bottle with 2 fingers headspace, cap, wait 2 weeks

**Tupelo Honey:** Harvested only in northern Florida and southern Georgia; sweet, spicy character worth its premium price.

### Metheglin: Winter Spiced Mead

*Source: [AHA - Winter Spiced Mead](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/winter-spiced-mead/) by Ken Schramm*

A medium-to-sweet still metheglin with layers of complexity that evolve over years of aging.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (19 L) |
| Style | Metheglin (Spiced Mead) |
| Sweetness | Medium to sweet |
| Carbonation | Still |

**Ingredients:**
- 20 lb (9.07 kg) Honey (raspberry, orange, or blackberry blossom recommended)
- 5+ gallons (19 L) Spring water
- 7 g (1.4 tsp) Ground cinnamon
- 7 g (1.4 tsp) Ground nutmeg
- 7 g (1.4 tsp) Ground dried ginger (optional)
- 1 g (0.2 tsp) Ground cloves (optional)
- 1 small vanilla bean

**Yeast:** Lalvin 71B-1122 or Wyeast 1056 (15g rehydrated)

**Nutrients (TOSNA-style schedule):**
- At lag phase (8-12 hours): 4g DAP + 3g Fermaid K
- Daily for 3 more days: 2g DAP + 2g Fermaid K

**Process:**
1. Mix all ingredients thoroughly
2. Aerate vigorously with mixer or immersion blender
3. Create slightly more than 5 gallons (to fill secondary)
4. Ferment 21-28 days (may be longer with 1056)
5. Transfer to secondary when fermentation completes
6. Age 6-12 months minimum
7. Fine with Sparkolloid if needed
8. Bottle only after all activity ceases

**Ken Schramm's Tip:** This mead will have layers of complexity and will evolve subtly as it matures.
